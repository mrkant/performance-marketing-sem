Advertising in Japan hub › Agency governance

Hiring an agency is not buying media.

The cost hub explains what advertising costs in Japan. This guide covers the next problem: who signs, what is in scope, which claims are pass or fail, and how you leave. Five stages, each asked as who, what, when, where, why and how, across strategy, law, money, operations and proof.

Where to start

Entering Japan

Headquarters wants a partner. The first risk is hiring a translator of the global plan.

The five stages

Open a stage to see the six questions at five depths, the failure to avoid, the risks and the documents.

01Define the business requirementClarity · An approved internal requirement, before any agency is contacted.

Most bad appointments start here: a company asks for pitches before it can say which problem the agency is supposed to solve, which entity will sign, and which result it will not blame on the agency.

Typical failure. “We need a media agency for Japan” is not a scope. It produces incomparable proposals.

Who: Who is accountable, and who only advises?

Strategic
Name the business owner. Market entry, a weak Japanese subsidiary, a failed incumbent and a performance problem are different appointments.
Legal
Decide the contracting entity: Japanese subsidiary, overseas parent, distributor or regional marketing company. That choice drives tax, governing law and who is responsible for advertising representations.
Commercial
Name who may commit money. An agency manager must not raise media spend because a campaign “is working”.
Operational
Name the day-to-day owner, Japanese-copy approver, claim reviewer and after-hours contact. Do not assume a senior executive will do this.
Measurement
Separate what the agency controls from what the company controls: site, price, stock, service. A responsibility matrix stops both sides blaming the other for every miss.

What: What is actually being bought?

Strategic
Say whether the agency creates the Japan strategy or executes one the company already has.
Legal
List the regimes in scope: the Act against Unjustifiable Premiums and Misleading Representations, stealth-marketing disclosure, the Act on the Protection of Personal Information, copyright, and sector rules where they apply. Outsourcing does not move the advertiser’s statutory duty.
Commercial
Break the buy into strategy, creative, media, influencers, production, reporting and compliance. Volume matters: “social management” is not a specification; twenty posts still needs formats.
Operational
For each channel, say whether the agency plans, buys, optimises, reports, or only coordinates.
Measurement
List the objects the company must receive: plans, calendars, editable files, signed creator agreements, claim evidence, raw exports and a handover pack.

When: When must each decision happen?

Strategic
Match the clock to the job: market entry, a launch, a season, or a standing programme. Do not use a launch date to skip legal review.
Legal
Reserve time for claim checks, Japanese localisation, rights clearance, influencer approval and landing-page review. Cosmetics, health and financial services need more of it.
Commercial
Build a real procurement calendar: requirement, RFI, RFP, clarification, scoring, references, negotiation, signature. Illustrative, not a statutory timetable.
Operational
Set start dates for access, tracking, creative, compliance review and launch separately.
Measurement
Propose review points before signature: 30 days, 60 days, 90-day pilot, quarterly, annual. These are governance intervals, not legal requirements.

Where: Where does the work, data and law sit?

Strategic
Japan is not one audience. Tokyo professionals and households across prefectures are different plans.
Legal
Decide governing law, forum, controlling language and whether work may be subcontracted outside Japan.
Commercial
State currency, invoice destination, consumption-tax treatment and whether media is billed by the platform, the agency or another intermediary.
Operational
Ask where files, accounts, customer mail and analytics are actually processed. A bilingual account lead is not a Japanese copywriter.
Measurement
Follow the journey past the post. An Instagram ad that lands on an overseas checkout is still the company’s problem.

Why: Why is an agency the right instrument?

Strategic
Name the business problem in a sentence a board can reject. “Improve marketing” will not survive contact with a proposal.
Legal
An agency can tighten control. It cannot erase the company’s responsibility for what is published in its name.
Commercial
Compare agency cost with hiring: salary, recruiting, tools, production, specialist review, management time. The cheapest line is often not the cheaper system.
Operational
If the company already has the capability, buy the missing specialist, not a full retainer.
Measurement
Write the expected return as qualified reach, branded search, traffic, sales, acquisition cost, retention or lower compliance exposure — and say which of those the agency can actually move.

How: How will the company control it?

Strategic
Turn the answers into one Agency Requirement Specification: background, Japan objective, current state, capabilities, scope, consumers, channels, deliverables, compliance, budget, reporting, term, proposed KPIs, evaluation method and timetable.
Legal
Mark which requirements are pass/fail: compliance policy, data terms, subcontractor disclosure, audit, ownership or licence of deliverables.
Commercial
Put a budget range in the document so agencies price the same assumption. Hide the ceiling only if procurement policy requires it — then still fix the scope.
Operational
Attach a RACI, an authority matrix and an escalation list. Conflicting instructions from marketing and legal are a client failure.
Measurement
State how proposals will be scored before they arrive. Changing the scorecard after the pitch is how favourites win.

Risks

  • Pitch theatre. Agencies present well against a vague brief. The comparison is then taste, not fit.
  • Wrong capability. A PR firm asked to buy media, or a studio asked to own conversion, fails in a way the contract did not describe.
  • Split accountability. Several departments brief the agency. Claims go live without the reviewer who was supposed to see them.
  • Entity confusion. The group brand is famous. A different company sends the invoice and holds the accounts.

Documents

  • Internal stakeholder register
  • Decision-authority matrix
  • RACI for the agency relationship
  • Budget approval rule
  • Escalation and incident contacts
  • Agency requirement specification

Stage output: an internal agency requirement that later becomes the RFI and RFP.

02Research and evaluate the marketConfidence · A shortlist backed by evidence, not by the best website.

The Japanese market has full-service groups, digital buyers, social managers, influencer shops, production studios and PR firms. They are not substitutes. This stage checks who would actually do the work.

Typical failure. Selecting an agency because it claims Japan capability, or because a regional office once ran a campaign nearby.

Who: Who is accountable, and who only advises?

Strategic
Look at the legal entity that would sign, not the group brand on the leave-behind.
Legal
Check registered name, address, representative and whether a subsidiary or subcontractor does the work you think you are buying.
Commercial
Meet the people who can refuse extra spend and who own compliance, not only the team that presents.
Operational
Ask for names and status of the account director, planner, buyer, Japanese writer, producer, analyst and community lead. Employee, contractor or subcontractor is a different risk.
Measurement
Take references from comparable clients and ask about delivery, transparency, team churn, compliance, crisis and what happened at exit — not whether they “liked” the agency.

What: What is actually being bought?

Strategic
Demand evidence of research, segmentation, channel choice and budget logic. Generic lines about Japanese consumers are not a strategy.
Legal
Test stealth-marketing process, claim review, music and talent permissions, and data handling. A promise of compliance is not a process.
Commercial
Read Japanese copy in the work samples. Visual craft can hide weak language.
Operational
Check current tools: GA4, tag management, conversion APIs, UTMs, CRM, naming. Ask them to trace your journey, not recite a platform list.
Measurement
Ask for a sample report that explains what changed and what decision it requires. Screenshots of followers are not a report.

When: When must each decision happen?

Strategic
Look across three to five years for a pattern, not one awarded campaign.
Legal
Confirm the compliance reviewer is on the proposed team now, not “available from the network”.
Commercial
Ask when the named people can start. Senior names in a pitch are often booked.
Operational
Test readiness for Japanese commercial seasons — New Year, Valentine’s, Golden Week, summer, Obon, year-end — and the lead time those require.
Measurement
Ask whether the same team can still run the account if spend doubles or a second brand is added.

Where: Where does the work, data and law sit?

Strategic
Use Japanese corporate sites, group structures, specialist networks, professional referrals and live campaign credits. A portfolio credit is not proof they did every part.
Legal
Ask where personal data and launch files are stored, which subcontractors see them, and which overseas systems receive them.
Commercial
Separate Japan-only work from a regional hub that adapts English assets.
Operational
Visit or inspect the actual Japanese-language operation if the fee assumes it.
Measurement
Prefer references in a similar regulatory environment. A fashion win does not prove pharmaceutical discipline.

Why: Why is an agency the right instrument?

Strategic
Shortlist for the capability in the requirement, not for fame.
Legal
An agency that cannot show disclosure, claims and rights workflows is a poor fit for a regulated or multinational client, however strong the creative is.
Commercial
A lower fee with undisclosed production margin and rebates is not a better offer. That test belongs in the next stage; flag it now.
Operational
Subcontracting is acceptable. Hidden subcontracting is not.
Measurement
Do not let a high creative score cancel a failed compliance or data gate.

How: How will the company control it?

Strategic
Score 1–5 on Japanese strategy, execution, team, compliance, measurement, commercial transparency, references and communication. Publish the weights before scoring.
Legal
Pass/fail gates: compliance policy, data terms, subcontractor disclosure, financial transparency, audit, and ownership or licence of deliverables.
Commercial
Keep a written reason for every score. Procurement memory is short; disputes are not.
Operational
Use the question bank. Do not ask all of it in one meeting.
Measurement
The illustrative weights in the scorecard are a procurement model, not an industry standard. Change them to match the brief — before the pitches.

Risks

  • Borrowed credentials. The case study was the client’s, or another agency’s media buy.
  • Vanished team. The people in the room are not the people on the account.
  • Compliance theatre. The deck says “we follow the law”. Nobody can walk through a disclosure on a Story or a live stream.
  • Data fog. Audience files move to an overseas tool the company has not named.

Documents

  • Corporate profile and registration check
  • Proposed team and employment status
  • Subcontractor register
  • Reference notes
  • Case-study contribution notes
  • Comparative scorecard

Stage output: a documented shortlist, a score and a record of who was excluded and why.

03Procure, negotiate and contractProtection · A decision memo, a master agreement and a statement of work that describe the same job.

Three quotations and the lowest retainer is not a procurement. Agencies price different hours, different media models and different rights. Compare them on one scope.

Typical failure. Signing a twelve-month retainer to make a launch date, with video “probably included” and accounts in the agency’s name.

Who: Who is accountable, and who only advises?

Strategic
Marketing judges whether the proposal solves the Stage 1 problem. Procurement runs the process. Neither does the other’s job.
Legal
Counsel reviews parties, liability, advertising duties, creator terms, IP, confidentiality, personal information, subcontracting, termination and disputes.
Commercial
Finance tests affordability, tax, payment, media cash flow and the assumptions behind any return figure.
Operational
Negotiate with the people who will deliver: account, media, creative, operations, commercial. A sales promise the operators have not heard is not a term.
Measurement
The selection memorandum names who approved the appointment and which conditions are still open.

What: What is actually being bought?

Strategic
The RFP states the commercial problem, not “please run social”. Ask for thinking, not a free finished campaign.
Legal
Require a compliance approach: AUPMR, stealth marketing, sector rules if relevant, APPI, copyright, music, platform rules, disclosure and escalation.
Commercial
Force an itemised price: strategy, setup, retainer, production, media fees, media spend, creators, commissions, licences, software, compliance, extras, consumption tax.
Operational
Ask for a 30/60/90-day plan. It shows whether they understand the workload.
Measurement
Separate service levels the agency can control from outcomes several parties affect. On-time delivery can be an obligation. Sales cannot be a guarantee.

When: When must each decision happen?

Strategic
An illustrative path for a moderately complex appointment runs about nine weeks from brief to signature. Regulated and cross-border work takes longer.
Legal
Do not compress contract review into the week before a media booking.
Commercial
Clarify assumptions in writing before scoring. Otherwise you are scoring different jobs.
Operational
A pilot of three or six months is often wiser than a year, where the agency will accept it.
Measurement
Define the pilot’s learning period before anyone treats week-three ROAS as a verdict.

Where: Where does the work, data and law sit?

Strategic
Write the geographic scope: Japan only, selected markets, or Japan adaptation of a global plan.
Legal
Name the entities, the governing law, the language that controls and how notices are given. Counsel should shape cross-border structures.
Commercial
State currency, invoice path, bank, consumption tax, payment terms and foreign-exchange treatment.
Operational
Prefer client-owned advertising accounts, with the agency on controlled access, wherever the platform allows it.
Measurement
Say where raw data must remain readable by the company after the contract ends.

Why: Why is an agency the right instrument?

Strategic
Negotiation exists to remove ambiguity, not to win a discount on an undefined service.
Legal
Indemnity language does not replace statutory compliance. A clause that says “the agency is responsible for all violations” does not remove the advertiser’s exposure.
Commercial
A 15% fee on media is not comparable to a fixed retainer until both are fully costed.
Operational
The company needs room to change channels, pause spend, add a brand or leave.
Measurement
Without a recorded scoring method, the renewal conversation in a year has nothing to stand on.

How: How will the company control it?

Strategic
Run three evaluations: technical fit, total cost, and risk. Do not collapse them into one “feel” score.
Legal
The agreement should cover parties, scope, deliverables, timetable, fees, media authority, approvals, compliance, creators, IP, data, confidentiality, subcontractors, reporting, audit, liability, insurance where needed, termination, transition and disputes.
Commercial
Creator spend is five different numbers: talent fee, agency coordination, management commission, usage rights, amplification rights, plus shipping. Price them apart.
Operational
Moral rights under Japanese copyright law are not transferable. Get the permissions you need and a carefully drafted non-exercise covenant where that is appropriate. Do not assume assignment solves it.
Measurement
Put controllable SLAs and shared commercial targets in different columns of the KPI schedule. The scorecard’s example weights are a model, not a norm.

Risks

  • Hidden scope. Ten posts were priced. Filming, subtitles and usage were not.
  • Account captivity. The company cannot open its own ad accounts or export audiences.
  • Rebate silence. Platform incentives and production margin sit outside the proposal.
  • Fake guarantees. A sales target is written as if the agency controlled price, stock and the website.

Documents

  • RFP and clarification log
  • Technical scorecard
  • Total-cost comparison
  • Selection memorandum
  • Master services agreement and statement of work
  • Fee schedule, SLA schedule and KPI schedule

Stage output: scored proposals, negotiated fees, an executed agreement and an approved statement of work.

04Onboard, govern and controlControl · A working system: access, money, approvals, evidence and escalation.

Signature is not a relationship. Agencies fail in the first month because nobody owns approval, account access, or the rule that nothing publishes before the required review is recorded.

Typical failure. A shared chat is the only record of approval, and the agency is the only admin on the ad account.

Who: Who is accountable, and who only advises?

Strategic
An executive sponsor sets priority and hears escalations. They should not approve ordinary posts.
Legal
Give legal and compliance a defined seat by risk type. Do not copy every draft to every department.
Commercial
Finance verifies invoices. Marketing must not be the only reader of pass-through spend.
Operational
Confirm the team you bought is the team you received. Require notice of director, strategist, creative lead or material subcontractor changes.
Measurement
Publish a RACI. Example pattern: agency responsible for content, client accountable, legal consulted, finance informed — then rewrite it for the real company.

What: What is actually being bought?

Strategic
Brief the agency with guidelines, approved claims, prohibited claims, history and what a crisis sounds like.
Legal
Build a disclosure control for sponsored posts, gifting, affiliates, employee posts, reposts, testimonials, amplification, video, Stories and live streams.
Commercial
Write the money rules: annual budget, monthly mix, extra media, influencer quotes, unplanned production, and whether money may move between platforms without approval.
Operational
Workflow: brief, draft, internal review, legal review where required, recorded approval, publish, monitor, archive. No approval record, no publish.
Measurement
The monthly report must answer what happened, why, what to change and what decision the client owes. Not a gallery of likes.

When: When must each decision happen?

Strategic
Match the calendar to risk. A quiet corporate account does not need the monitoring of a high-volume consumer brand.
Legal
Check the live unit, not only the file. Captions truncate. Videos are entered mid-way. Stories are a sequence of frames.
Commercial
Reconcile every month: invoice, platform spend, creators, production, software, tax, budget, balance.
Operational
A usable cadence is often: monitoring as agreed, weekly operations, optimisation on a set rhythm, monthly money and performance, quarterly strategy, annual supplier review.
Measurement
Version the asset. You should be able to point at the draft, the approval, the live file and any later edit.

Where: Where does the work, data and law sit?

Strategic
Instructions and approvals live in a company system. A private messaging app is a poor sole record.
Legal
Keep briefs, drafts, comments, finals, contracts, invoices, reports and disclosure evidence in one place the company controls.
Commercial
Hold admin rights on Meta, Google, TikTok, YouTube, X, LINE and analytics wherever the product allows. Agency access is delegated.
Operational
Know where customer and campaign data sit, including overseas processors. The Personal Information Protection Commission’s guidance is the reference for APPI, not the agency’s privacy page.
Measurement
If production or community work is overseas, write the quality and data controls before the first brief.

Why: Why is an agency the right instrument?

Strategic
Governance is how the company stays able to operate if the agency’s staff leave.
Legal
A compliant concept becomes non-compliant when a creator changes a line or the agency edits a caption after approval.
Commercial
Unlimited spending authority is not “agility”.
Operational
Japan’s social environment can enlarge a complaint quickly. The agency does not issue corporate statements unless authorised.
Measurement
Without records, every dispute becomes a memory contest.

How: How will the company control it?

Strategic
Run separate workstreams: disclosure, creators, media, production, community, reporting, money, data, document control, subcontractors.
Legal
The Consumer Affairs Agency’s position is contextual. There is no universal statutory font size, no universal three-to-five-second video rule, and no universal fifteen-minute spoken interval. Persistent labels can be a conservative internal control. Do not write them into policy as if they were the statute.
Commercial
Keep an influencer register: identity, audience, fit, fee, contract, disclosure check, usage rights. Follower count is not a selection method.
Operational
Secondary use is a separate right. A sponsored post does not automatically allow paid amplification, use of the creator’s handle, worldwide distribution or an open-ended term.
Measurement
Reconcile approved budget, platform cost, agency fee, credits and remaining balance. Ask for reasonable support for pass-through costs.

Risks

  • Approval by silence. The post went live because nobody objected in a chat thread.
  • Caption drift. The approved line was edited after legal review.
  • Single admin. Only the agency can pause spend or recover the account.
  • Rights gap. The company boosts a creator film it never licensed for ads.

Documents

  • Approved-claims register
  • Account access register
  • Budget authority matrix
  • Content approval workflow
  • Influencer register and creative asset register
  • Incident and escalation matrix

Stage output: workflows, access, budget rules, a compliance record and a reporting cadence.

05Measure, renew or leaveValue · Evidence for keeping, fixing or ending the relationship.

An agency can hit every meeting and still not be worth the money. Another can grow sales and still be unacceptable on disclosure or spend. Judge both questions.

Typical failure. Waiting for month twelve, or cancelling in month one, against targets nobody baselined.

Who: Who is accountable, and who only advises?

Strategic
Management decides whether to expand, replace or take work in-house. Marketing does not make that call alone.
Legal
Legal reviews disclosure accuracy, claims, IP, data incidents and contract breaches as their own score, not a footnote to ROAS.
Commercial
Finance reviews budget use, invoice accuracy, fee versus media, and whether “return” survives margin, returns and discounts.
Operational
Procurement tests contract compliance, price realism and whether the market has moved.
Measurement
The agency is not the only source of truth. Use platform data, company analytics, commerce or CRM, invoices and the compliance file.

What: What is actually being bought?

Strategic
Seven families are enough: delivery, reach, engagement, video, traffic and conversion, compliance, and the commercial relationship.
Legal
Compliance targets can be strict on controllable acts: reviewed before publish, correct disclosure, no unapproved claims, evidence kept. Separate those from clerical report errors.
Commercial
ROAS is not profit. Say so in the review pack.
Operational
Count team stability, responsiveness and whether recommendations changed the next month’s work.
Measurement
Define each metric before the pilot. Platforms do not share one definition of a view or of engagement.

When: When must each decision happen?

Strategic
30 days checks onboarding. 60 days checks early execution. 90 days judges the pilot. Then monthly, quarterly, six months, annual.
Legal
A serious disclosure or spend breach is reviewed when it happens, not at the quarterly business review.
Commercial
Renewal is a decision. An auto-renew clause is not a strategy.
Operational
Do not declare a commercial failure inside an agreed learning window. Do not hide an operational failure inside that window either.
Measurement
Corrective plans need a named owner, a date and a way to verify. “We will improve” is not a plan.

Where: Where does the work, data and law sit?

Strategic
Read results in the Japanese commercial calendar, not only against a global weekly template.
Legal
Evidence sits with the company: approval files, live screenshots, claim support, incident logs.
Commercial
Match reported media spend to invoices and to the platform, in the same currency treatment you contracted.
Operational
Exit is a place as much as a date: accounts, files, rights, audiences and unspent balances have to move.
Measurement
Keep direct access so a deteriorating relationship cannot switch off the numbers.

Why: Why is an agency the right instrument?

Strategic
The review justifies the next period of spend. It is also the only orderly way to replace someone.
Legal
Repeated compliance failure is a reason to leave even if the creative is admired.
Commercial
Fees that were fair at signature may not be fair after the scope quietly grew.
Operational
The company should become more capable, not more dependent on a supplier that holds every login.
Measurement
Judge the agency against the contract and the agreed targets, not against a new expectation invented after the campaign.

How: How will the company control it?

Strategic
Use a weighted monthly scorecard, then override it for serious failures. Excellent creative does not pass unauthorised spend.
Legal
Termination triggers worth writing: material breach, repeated SLA failure, serious regulatory breach, unauthorised spend, confidentiality or data incidents, insolvency, hidden subcontracting, unacceptable conflicts. Also consider termination for convenience with an agreed notice and cost.
Commercial
Before renewal, re-benchmark fees and ask whether a different model — specialist plus in-house, or a lead agency — would now be better.
Operational
The exit list: ad accounts, admin rights, structures, history, audiences, tracking, source files, exports, creator contracts, licences, calendars, approvals, invoices, unspent media, subcontractors, and confirmation that data is returned or deleted.
Measurement
A quarterly review covers objectives, spend, creative, audience, conversion, market change, competitors, risk, resourcing, next strategy and the budget ask. The agency states what it learned, not only what it shipped.

Risks

  • Activity mistaken for value. Every post shipped. The commercial problem from Stage 1 is unchanged.
  • Late judgement. The first serious review is the renewal notice period.
  • Captive history. The agency leaves with the only copy of audiences, files and approvals.
  • Scorecard camouflage. A high average hides a failed gate on disclosure or money.

Documents

  • Monthly scorecard
  • Quarterly business review pack
  • Corrective action record
  • Annual supplier evaluation
  • Renewal recommendation
  • Handover checklist and deletion or return note

Stage output: a performance record and a renewal, remedy or exit plan.

Who you might be hiring

Full-service advertising agency

Strategy, creative, media planning, buying and campaign management in one relationship. Large Japanese advertising groups are the familiar version. Useful when the company wants one accountable lead and has the governance to direct it.

Digital media agency

Paid search, display, social, programmatic, analytics and optimisation. Hire this for execution and measurement, not for corporate reputation or television franchise deals, unless the team actually has that work.

Social management agency

Channel strategy, calendars, Japanese copy, publishing, community and reporting. A posting retainer is not a media-buying contract and not an influencer legal function unless the scope says so.

Influencer marketing agency

Creator identification, fees, contracts, disclosures and reporting. The advertiser still owns how those relationships are governed. Follower count is a weak selector.

Creative and production agency

Concepts, design, photography, video, Japanese copy and assets. Price usage, talent, music and revisions separately. Paying for a film is not the same as owning it.

PR and communications agency

Media relations, publicity, reputation and crisis communications. Do not assume a PR retainer includes paid media, performance tracking or marketplace advertising.

Questions to ask before you sign

Corporate identity
  • What is the exact Japanese legal entity name that would sign?
  • How long has that company operated in Japan?
  • Which services are delivered by employees, and which are subcontracted?
  • Who is the executive sponsor, and who manages the account day to day?
  • How many current clients are in our category, and can you name comparable references?
Japanese market expertise
  • How do you research Japanese consumers, and what data do you actually use?
  • How do you adapt an overseas brand without only translating it?
  • Who writes the Japanese, and who edits it?
  • What have you done with LINE, X, Instagram, TikTok, YouTube, Google and Yahoo! JAPAN — separately?
  • How do you judge whether localisation worked?
Strategy and media
  • Which channels would you recommend for this brief, and which would you refuse?
  • How would you split a defined budget between brand and response?
  • What would you need from us before a plan is real?
  • How would the plan change if the first quarter missed the agreed learning targets?
  • How do you treat Japanese seasonal peaks in production and booking lead times?
People
  • Who will do the work, and are they employees?
  • How many other accounts does the proposed manager hold?
  • What happens when that person leaves, and can we approve a material replacement?
  • Will senior people attend quarterly reviews?
  • How is account knowledge stored somewhere other than one person’s head?
Compliance
  • How do you decide that a representation is the advertiser’s under the stealth-marketing designation?
  • Who checks the final caption, the video, the Story frames and the live stream?
  • How are product claims substantiated, and who may not approve them?
  • How do you clear music, talent, fonts and third-party marks, and for which territory and term?
  • Show an anonymised example of a disclosure failure and what you did next.
Money
  • What is inside the retainer, and what is a change request?
  • How is the media fee calculated, and on which base?
  • Do you receive rebates, credits or platform incentives, and how are they disclosed?
  • Are creator fees passed through at cost?
  • May we audit supporting records for pass-through spend?
Performance
  • Which KPIs do you recommend, and which will you not guarantee?
  • What baseline do you need before a target is honest?
  • How do you define engagement, CPA and ROAS for this account?
  • How do you check platform conversions against our analytics or store?
  • What do you do, contractually, when a service level is missed?
Data and the contract
  • Who owns the advertising accounts and the analytics property?
  • What personal data will you touch, where is it stored, and who else sees it?
  • Are there overseas transfers?
  • Can we begin with a pilot?
  • What is handed over on exit, and by when?

Disclosure control: can a stranger tell this is an ad?

Since 1 October 2023 hard-to-identify business representations are treated as misleading under Japan's stealth-marketing designation. The Consumer Affairs Agency states that the regulated party is the advertiser. This checklist is an internal control for one asset, not a finding of compliance.

There is no universal statutory font size, no required opening card and no required spoken disclosure interval. A company may still adopt such labels as policy. Call that a control, not law.

Legal notes

General information as at November 2026, not legal advice. Confirm the current text with a qualified Japanese lawyer.

Who the stealth-marketing rule actually binds

From 1 October 2023, representations that are difficult for ordinary consumers to identify as a business operator’s own are treated as misleading under the Act against Unjustifiable Premiums and Misleading Representations. The Consumer Affairs Agency states that the regulated party is the business supplying the goods or services — the advertiser. Third parties such as influencers commissioned to advertise are not the target of that designation. Cabinet Office Notification No. 19 of 28 March 2023 is the designation. The useful procurement question is whether the advertiser determined the content, not whether a hashtag was typed.

Consumer Affairs Agency — stealth marketing ↗

Do not teach a 3% surcharge as the stealth-marketing penalty

The Agency’s overview page describes the designation and who it regulates. It does not state that a stealth-marketing violation automatically produces an administrative surcharge of 3% of sales. Surcharge orders under the Act are a separate instrument, attached to certain misleading representations when statutory conditions are met. Measure orders are the tool practitioners associate with the designation itself. Collapsing these into one percentage is how a serious guide becomes unreliable. Check the current CAA FAQ before a board paper or a contract recital uses a figure.

Consumer Affairs Agency ↗

Disclosure is contextual

Terms such as advertising, promotion or PR can be part of a clear disclosure. They are examples, not a magic token. Readability, colour, placement, video entry and platform chrome all matter. There is no statutory minimum font size, no statutory three-to-five-second opening slate, and no statutory rule that someone must speak a disclosure every fifteen minutes. A company may still adopt persistent labels as an internal control. Label that choice as policy.

CAA operational material ↗

Personal information is a data-flow question

Hashed emails, advertising identifiers, cookies and audience files are not “anonymous because we hashed them”. Ask for categories, purposes, roles, subprocessors, storage, overseas transfers, access, retention and deletion. The Personal Information Protection Commission publishes the guidance that should be matched to the actual flow. An agency privacy policy is not that analysis.

Personal Information Protection Commission ↗

Freelancers are a missing workstream

The Act on Ensuring Proper Transactions Involving Specified Entrusted Business Operators — often called the Freelance Act — took effect on 1 November 2024. It can matter when an agency or the hiring company commissions qualifying freelancers: writers, designers, photographers, editors, individual creators. It deals with disclosing transaction terms, payment timing and certain prohibited practices. Not every contractor relationship is in scope. The Japan Fair Trade Commission’s materials are the place to check the current boundary.

Japan Fair Trade Commission ↗

Copyright, moral rights and tax

Paying for work does not silently transfer every right you will later want, including paid amplification and territory. Author’s moral rights are not transferable; plan permissions and non-exercise language with counsel rather than a one-line assignment. Japan’s standard consumption tax rate is 10%, with exceptions and different treatment for some cross-border supplies. Where the Qualified Invoice System applies, ask for the registration number. Do not assume an overseas group company invoices like a domestic registered vendor.
