Advertising and marketing glossary for Japan: English and Japanese terms

By Shashikant · Updated 19 November 2026 · How this hub is made and checked

⚡ In one minute

Dozens of advertising terms in English and Japanese, with readings, so you can follow quotes and media kits.

A plain-English dictionary of terms you will meet in quotes, media kits and agency meetings in Japan, with the Japanese term and a reading in Roman letters.

This glossary and the surrounding pages are maintained as an independent educational resource. They are not a substitute for current rate cards, legal advice or a detailed discussion of your specific situation.

  • Reach and frequency — How many people see the message and how often. Critical for awareness media; less central for pure response campaigns.
  • GRP / gross rating point — A traditional television planning unit. Still used in conversations even as digital measurement grows.
  • First-party data and owned channels — Information and relationships the brand controls (site, LINE, CRM). Increasingly important as platform data becomes more restricted.
  • Creative testing — Systematically trying variations of message, format and offer. On many digital platforms the creative is the main driver of efficiency.
  • Attribution window — The time period in which a conversion is credited to an ad. Different windows produce different stories about what worked.
  • Fully loaded cost — Media + production + fees + internal time + expected returns or support cost. The number finance usually cares about more than the media line alone.
  • Compliance and representation — Rules around claims, premiums, labelling and disclosure (including influencer disclosure). Ignoring them creates both legal and trust risk.

Practical terms you will meet in quotes and meetings

Scenarios on this site are invented teaching constructs. They use round numbers to show decision logic, trade-offs and common failure patterns. They are not case studies of real companies and not predictions of results. Their purpose is to train judgement.

Teaching examples vs real cases

Advertising multiplies whatever the business already delivers. Strong creative and media can accelerate a good product and service; they can also accelerate disappointment if the website, support language, claims or fulfilment are weak. Before large awareness spend, check that the rest of the system can absorb attention without damaging trust.

Brand, service and experience as multipliers

Some media are bought primarily to make more people know the brand or keep it top-of-mind. Others are bought primarily to generate measurable response. Mixing the two without clarity about which job each medium is doing leads to confused expectations and unfair evaluation. Decide the job first, then choose the medium and the success measure that match the job.

Awareness vs conversion roles

Search and many social platforms can show cost per acquisition with reasonable confidence. Train posters, television and much out-of-home mainly show reach, frequency and later effects on search behaviour or brand preference. Forcing every medium into the same last-click ROAS formula systematically undervalues awareness media and overvalues the final click. Good decision-making accepts that some real value will remain outside the dashboard.

What can be measured cleanly vs what cannot

The money paid to a media owner or platform is rarely the full cost of getting a result. Creative, landing experience, tracking, agency or management fees, Japanese-language support, fulfilment capacity and returns all sit in the same system. A cheap media buy that drives traffic into a weak experience is still expensive. A more costly placement that is backed by a coherent offer and ready service can be efficient.

Media cost is only part of the system

Most numbers you will see in guides (including this one) are published ranges or agency-listed examples. They show scale. They are not the price you will be quoted. Real prices move with season, inventory, volume, creative requirements and negotiation. Treat every figure as a way to judge whether a medium is in the right order of magnitude for your situation, then request current quotes.
