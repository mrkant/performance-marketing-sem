How to choose a marketing agency in Japan

By Shashikant · Updated 19 November 2026 · How this hub is made and checked

⚡ In one minute

Ask what is included each month, who does the work, who owns the accounts and data, and how success is measured. Walk away from guaranteed results.

Ask these questions

What exactly is included each month, and what is not? Who will do the work, and can I meet them? Who owns the ad accounts, analytics and content? What happens to my data if we stop? How is success measured, and which numbers will I see each month? Can you show experience in my industry? How are changes and extra work priced?

Red flags

Guaranteed rankings or sales, no explanation of what the fee covers, refusal to give you account access, long lock-in periods with no exit terms, copied or template reports, and pressure to decide quickly. An unusually low price usually means thin scope or heavy outsourcing.

Match the provider to your stage

A new business with a small budget often does best with a freelancer or small boutique and a clear, narrow goal. A growing company with several channels benefits from a specialist agency with strategy and reporting. A large brand needs integrated planning, brand protection and governance, which favours big agencies. Start with the channel closest to the sale, usually search ads or marketplace advertising, then add awareness channels once the basics convert.

  • Refusal to put account ownership in writing.
  • Pressure to commit to long lock-in periods before any results are visible.
  • Reporting that is only platform screenshots with no commentary or recommendations.
  • Creative that ignores Japanese language and cultural context.
  • Claims that “we can guarantee ROI” without defining the terms and the data source.

Red flags

An agency that only thinks about media efficiency can still damage the brand if creative or claims are off-tone, or if landing pages and follow-up journeys are neglected. The best partners treat advertising as one part of a larger system that includes the website, service language and fulfilment. Ask how they will work with your internal teams or other suppliers on those pieces.

Brand, service and experience implications

Some agencies optimise for the metrics that are easiest to report rather than the ones that matter to the business. Agree in advance what “success” means for your category: new customers, repeat purchase, cost per qualified lead, brand search lift, or something else. If the agency’s dashboard only shows platform-reported conversions, push for business-level numbers as well. Platform metrics and business metrics are not the same thing.

How agencies affect what you can measure

Ask for a written scope and a sample report from a comparable client (anonymised is fine). Vague answers on ownership or measurement are early warning signs.

  • What exactly is included each month, and what is billed as extras?
  • Who will do the work, and can I meet them before we start?
  • Who owns the ad accounts, analytics properties, creative files and customer data?
  • How are results reported, and which decisions will those reports drive?
  • What is the exit process, and how long does it take to move accounts cleanly?
  • How do you handle Japanese compliance (claims, disclosure, pharmaceuticals, food, etc.)?

Questions that separate competent partners from the rest

A useful agency relationship is transparent about scope, ownership of assets, and how decisions are made. You should know who is doing the work day to day, how often you will see numbers, and what happens when results are below target. The cheapest quote is rarely the most expensive problem; unclear ownership of accounts and data is.
