Types of advertising agencies in Japan

By Shashikant · Updated 19 November 2026 · How this hub is made and checked

⚡ In one minute

Japan's suppliers range from general agencies like Dentsu and Hakuhodo to internet specialists, boutiques and freelancers, and price follows the group.

Japanese marketing suppliers fall into four broad groups, and price follows the group.

General agencies

Dentsu and Hakuhodo sit at the top. They plan and buy across television, print, outdoor and digital, and suit large brands running integrated campaigns. Minimum engagements are large, and most of the value is in media access and planning, so small and mid-sized advertisers rarely work with them directly.

Internet-specialist agencies

Companies such as CyberAgent, Septeni and Opt built their business on online advertising. They manage search, display and social campaigns, often with in-house creative and analytics teams. They typically expect meaningful monthly budgets, and they often have close ties with the major platforms.

Consulting-style and boutique agencies

These firms focus on a channel (for example SEO or Amazon), an industry (beauty, real estate, recruiting) or a method (data and attribution). They are smaller, more flexible and usually more willing to work with mid-sized budgets.

Freelancers and very small teams

Freelancers handle much of the small-business work, particularly ad management, website building, social posting and SEO writing. They are usually the cheapest option, and the quality range is wide. Their limit is capacity and breadth: one person rarely covers strategy, creative, development and reporting at once.

A fifth category is worth knowing about: platform and tool vendors that bundle software with service, such as influencer matching platforms, subscription website builders and LINE extension tools. They lower the entry price by standardising the work.

Sources: published 2026 Japanese price guides (Imitsu, Meetsmore, Issoh, Atsoho, StockSun, Tatap, Pamxy, Ownly, Ecnomikata, NTT East). Ranges are indicative, last checked November 2026. This is an educational resource, not an offer of services.

The agency type also shapes how much attention the wider customer experience receives. A narrow performance shop may optimise ads while the post-click journey and service quality remain weak. A broader partner is more likely to notice the mismatch, but only if you brief them on the whole system, not only on media.

Brand and experience implications

Performance-oriented shops tend to live inside platform metrics and CPA. Brand and full-service shops may emphasise reach, brand lift and longer-term equity. Neither is automatically right. Define the business outcome you care about first, then choose a partner whose measurement habits match that outcome. A pure performance agency can under-invest in brand; a pure brand agency can under-invest in conversion discipline.

Measurement culture differs by type

  • Full-service / network — complex, multi-channel programmes, heavy compliance needs, or when you want one point of accountability.
  • Digital / performance specialists — paid media, measurement and conversion rate work where speed and data matter more than broad brand stewardship.
  • Creative boutiques — when the bottleneck is the idea or the film, not the media buy.
  • Freelancers and small teams — well-scoped tasks (a landing page, a content calendar, a technical SEO audit) where you can manage coordination yourself.

When each type usually fits

Full-service and large network agencies usually carry higher retainers and more process. Digital specialists and performance agencies often price closer to media volume or project scope. Creative boutiques charge for ideas and production quality. Freelancers and small teams can be cost-efficient for defined tasks but may lack breadth or backup. Matching the type of supplier to the type of problem is more important than finding the lowest day rate.
