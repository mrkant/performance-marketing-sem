How marketing agencies charge in Japan

By Shashikant · Updated 19 November 2026 · How this hub is made and checked

⚡ In one minute

Percentage of spend, retainers, project fees and performance fees reward different behaviour. Read the scope, minimum term and data ownership clauses first.

Understanding the pricing model is as important as knowing the price, because two quotes of the same size can mean very different things.

Percentage of ad spend

The most common model for advertising management. The standard fee is about 20% of ad spend , so a ¥300,000 monthly ad budget costs about ¥60,000 in management fees. Small agencies and freelancers often charge 10–15%. Because this model rewards spending more, it is worth asking what the agency does to improve efficiency rather than simply increase budget.

Minimum fees

Many agencies set a floor. If your ad budget is only ¥100,000, a minimum fee of ¥50,000 would mean you pay an effective 50% on top. At small budgets a fixed monthly fee, often ¥30,000 to ¥100,000 with a freelancer, can be better value.

Fixed monthly retainer

Typical for SEO, social media management, consulting and EC operations. You pay a set amount for a defined scope. The most important line in the quote is the scope: the same ¥150,000 per month can mean eight posts and a summary report, or filming, short-video editing, comment replies and detailed analysis.

Project fee

Used for website builds, landing pages, LINE account setup and one-off creative. The price depends on page count, design level, functions and revisions. Always check how many rounds of changes are included and who owns the files.

Performance-based pricing

Fees depend on results, such as leads, sales or links. It lowers upfront risk, and it is common with affiliate networks, some SEO providers and influencer platforms. The trade-off is that providers may chase easy conversions, and the definition of a "result" needs to be written precisely.

Hybrid pricing

A fixed base plus a share of results, common in marketplace management: for example 10–20% of sales plus a ¥50,000–150,000 monthly fee.

Contract terms and extra costs

Minimum contract periods of three to six months are normal for social and SEO work. Initial setup fees, account creation fees and creative production are often separate. Ad spend itself is paid to the platform, either directly or through the agency, so check which arrangement applies and who owns the ad accounts and data when the contract ends.

Sources: published 2026 Japanese price guides (Imitsu, Meetsmore, Issoh, Atsoho, StockSun, Tatap, Pamxy, Ownly, Ecnomikata, NTT East). Ranges are indicative, last checked November 2026. This is an educational resource, not an offer of services.

The headline fee is rarely the full cost. Add production, tools, stock photography or talent usage, landing-page work, and internal time. Ask for a worked example of a typical month so that “extras” are visible before they appear on an invoice.

Total cost of ownership

Commission on media can encourage higher spend. Pure retainers can encourage stability over aggression. Performance fees can encourage short-term metrics that ignore longer-term brand or margin effects. None of these is automatically wrong; each creates incentives. Understand the incentive before you sign, and align the contract with the outcome you actually want.

How the pricing model shapes behaviour

The cheapest total is not always the lowest risk. Clarity of scope and ownership often matters more than a small difference in fee.

  • Media cost versus management or retainer fee
  • What is included in creative and revisions
  • Reporting frequency and depth
  • Who owns accounts and data
  • Notice period and exit process
