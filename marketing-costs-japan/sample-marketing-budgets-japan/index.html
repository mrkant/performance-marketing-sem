Sample marketing budgets in Japan

By Shashikant · Updated 19 November 2026 · How this hub is made and checked

⚡ In one minute

Three worked budgets: a local business around ¥300,000 a month, a growing online brand around ¥1.5 million, and a foreign brand's first six months in Japan.

Small local business (about ¥300,000 per month)

Search ads at ¥150,000 with a freelancer charging ¥30,000–45,000, plus a basic LINE account and a simple, well-written landing page built once for around ¥150,000. Keep reporting simple: calls, bookings and cost per booking.

Growing online brand (about ¥1.5 million per month)

Ad spend of ¥800,000 with a 15–20% management fee (¥120,000–160,000), a social media retainer around ¥250,000, an SEO content programme around ¥200,000 and ¥100,000 for creative and testing. Revisit allocation quarterly based on cost per acquisition.

Foreign brand entering Japan (first six months)

Localised website or Shopify store (¥300,000–1 million one-off), marketplace onboarding if relevant, search and social ads with a management fee, a small influencer pilot of a few creators, and a compliance review of claims and labelling. Plan on testing before scaling, because early results teach you which messages work for Japanese buyers.

Sources: published 2026 Japanese price guides (Imitsu, Meetsmore, Issoh, Atsoho, StockSun, Tatap, Pamxy, Ownly, Ecnomikata, NTT East). Ranges are indicative, last checked November 2026. This is an educational resource, not an offer of services.

  • What is included in the agency or management fee, and what is billed extra?
  • How much is reserved for creative testing and refresh?
  • What happens to the plan if the first six weeks under-perform?
  • Which numbers will be reported weekly, and which decisions will those numbers drive?
  • Who owns the ad accounts and the data if the relationship ends?

Questions to ask of any proposed budget

A foreign DTC brand allocates ¥600,000 per month to Japan. ¥400,000 goes to media, ¥120,000 to management, and the rest to creative. After three months the cost per acquisition looks acceptable on paper, but the landing page is a translated home page, the creative is the same as the home-market ads, and there is no Japanese-language support path. Conversion rate stays low. Raising media spend without fixing the experience and the offer usually multiplies the waste. The first improvement is often not more budget; it is better alignment between the promise and the page, the service and the fulfilment.

Teaching example, not a real company.

Illustrative scenario: the “almost enough” budget

Small budgets force simple measurement: calls, form fills, cost per booking. Larger budgets can support proper experiments, creative testing and brand-lift studies. Match the measurement plan to the money. Spending ¥2 million a month while still judging success only by last-click conversions usually wastes information that the spend could have bought.

Linking budget size to measurement ambition

A budget that looks cheap on media can become expensive once these items are added. Ask for a full cost picture, not only the media line.

  • Creative production and ongoing refresh (especially video).
  • Landing-page or site improvements so traffic can convert.
  • Tracking, analytics and reporting setup.
  • Compliance review for claims, labelling and influencer disclosure.
  • Internal time: someone has to brief, approve, and act on the numbers.
