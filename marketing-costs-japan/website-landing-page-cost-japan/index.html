Website and landing page costs in Japan

By Shashikant · Updated 19 November 2026 · How this hub is made and checked

⚡ In one minute

Most company sites cost ¥300,000–1 million and a basic landing page ¥100,000–300,000. Ask who owns the files and what maintenance costs.

Corporate and service websites

Prices vary more than almost anywhere else. Agencies commonly charge from ¥100,000 to over ¥5 million, freelancers ¥50,000–300,000, and template or subscription services a few thousand yen per month. At one large Japanese marketplace the average corporate site cost about ¥956,000 while the median was ¥500,000, because a few expensive projects pull the average up. Most small and mid-sized company sites land at ¥300,000–1,000,000 . Recruiting sites with entry forms and staff stories run ¥500,000–1.5 million.

Landing pages

A basic landing page costs ¥100,000–300,000 . A sales page with professional copywriting runs ¥300,000–600,000, and one built for advertising with A/B testing ¥500,000–1 million. A good landing page can be worth the price: a page converting at 3% rather than 0.5% makes the same ad budget perform several times better.

What affects price

Number of pages, custom design versus templates, copywriting, photography, forms and booking functions, content management system, multilingual versions, SEO setup and the number of revision rounds. Ongoing costs include domain, hosting, security updates and content changes, often ¥10,000–50,000 per month for maintenance.

How the process works

Requirements and sitemap, design mock-ups, build, testing and launch, usually over six to twelve weeks for a corporate site and two to four weeks for a landing page.

Before you pay: what to ask and how to measure it

Confirm page count, revision rounds, who owns the design files and code, hosting and maintenance costs, and whether analytics and form tracking are set up. A site that cannot show where enquiries come from makes every later advertising decision harder.

Sources: published 2026 Japanese price guides (Imitsu, Meetsmore, Issoh, Atsoho, StockSun, Tatap, Pamxy, Ownly, Ecnomikata, NTT East). Ranges are indicative, last checked November 2026. This is an educational resource, not an offer of services.

The landing page is often the first sustained experience a person has with the brand after an ad. It should match the promise of the creative in tone, language and offer. Mismatches (different claims, different visual language, missing Japanese support paths) raise bounce rates and lower trust. Treat the page as part of the product experience, not only as a marketing asset.

Brand and experience implications

Instrument the page properly: analytics, conversion events, and ideally a way to connect visits to downstream business results. Without measurement, you cannot tell whether traffic problems or page problems are limiting results. Many “media” problems are actually landing-page problems.

Measurement

The page exists to turn attention into action. Speed, clarity of the offer, trust signals, mobile behaviour and form friction matter more than visual novelty. A cheaper page that is fast, clear and well-written in Japanese will usually outperform an expensive page that is slow or confusing. Budget for copy and user testing, not only for design and code.

Conversion and experience are the real product

A simple landing page built on a template by a freelancer and a custom, researched, multi-page site with complex integrations are different products. Quotes that look comparable on the surface often include very different scopes: research, copywriting in Japanese, design, development, CMS, analytics, ongoing maintenance and hosting. Always compare scope, not only the headline number.
