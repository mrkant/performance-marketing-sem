Paid media in Japan: channels, costs and how it works

By Shashikant · Updated 19 November 2026 · How this hub is made and checked

⚡ In one minute

Paid media is advertising you pay for by click, view or impression. In Japan, search ads are the largest single online type at about ¥1.28 trillion in 2025, with online video above ¥1 trillion. Google leads search, but LINE and Yahoo! JAPAN give a separate route to Japanese audiences.

Paid media means any placement you buy rather than earn: search ads, social ads, video ads, display banners, marketplace ads and sponsored content. This hub explains how it works in Japan, what the market looks like, and where decisions go wrong. It is a map first, and each branch links to a detailed guide.

Terms you will hear

Paid media is the broad category. PPC (pay per click) describes how many ads are charged. Search ads, called リスティング広告 in Japan, are text ads shown when someone searches. SEM is used inconsistently: some teams mean paid search only, others mean paid search plus SEO. When you brief an agency, write down which meaning you are using.

Who searches where

Google dominates search in Japan, but the split depends on device. StatCounter's September 2026 all-device figures show Google about 59%, Bing about 31% and Yahoo! about 7.6%. On mobile in August 2026, Google was about 82% and Yahoo! about 14%. On desktop in June 2026, Bing and Google were roughly level at about 46.7% and 45.9%.

Not verified StatCounter measures traffic, not ad inventory or buying power, and later desktop months may include a measurement break. How to choose where to start

Interpretation Start where people already want what you sell: search ads capture existing demand. Add video and social ads to create demand once the offer converts. Use marketplace ads if most of your sales happen on Amazon or Rakuten. Keep measurement simple enough to explain to a finance director: spend, orders, margin, and what would have happened without the ads.

Six common mistakes

Judging results by ROAS alone, when margin, returns and fees decide profit. Bidding on your own brand name without testing what you would earn anyway. Copying a campaign from another country without Japanese keyword research. Letting automation write claims that regulators treat as the advertiser's own. Splitting a small budget over too many channels. Signing an agency contract without account and data ownership.
