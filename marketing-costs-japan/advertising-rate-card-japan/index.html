Japan advertising rate card 2026: prices by channel, with sources and method

By Shashikant · Updated 19 November 2026 · How this hub is made and checked

⚡ In one minute

This rate card collects public prices for advertising in Japan from media owners, platforms, agencies and price guides, and marks each row A to D by how reliable it is. Only a few figures come straight from the seller. The method is shown below so you can check it.

Collected from public web sourcesAdvertising prices in Japan are scattered across platform pages, media-owner guides, agency lists and price comparisons. This page brings them together by category, shows where each number came from and how reliable it is, and ends with the method used to collect it. All prices are in yen and exclude 10% consumption tax unless stated.

Market context

Class ADentsu reports total advertising spend in Japan in 2025 of about ¥8.06 trillion, of which internet advertising was about ¥4.05 trillion (50.2%) and the four mass media about ¥2.30 trillion. Source: Dentsu summary.

What is not included

Agency fees, creative production, talent, localisation and tax are separate from media prices. A 20% agency fee and 10% consumption tax on a ¥1,000,000 media budget make the invoice about ¥1,320,000. Check whether quotes are tax-exclusive (税別) or tax-inclusive (税込).

How this table was built

1. Categories first. Channels were grouped the way advertisers buy them: auction-based digital, social and creators, television, transit and out-of-home, print and PR.

2. Public sources only. Figures come from pages anyone can open: platform pricing pages, media-owner guides, agency price lists and comparison sites. No private rate cards, paywalled data or behind-login pages were used.

3. Every row is recorded. For each figure the source, unit, tax status and date checked (November 2026) are noted. A row is class A only if the seller published it, class B if an agency or comparison source published it and it was confirmed elsewhere, class C if it is a rounded estimate across sources, and class D when no reliable number was found.

4. Conflicts are shown. Where sources disagree, the wider range is shown or the row is marked D rather than choosing one number.

5. Gaps are stated. Rows marked B (audit-reported) come from an independent audit of the media owner's own page. We have not yet opened each owner page ourselves, so confirm them before relying on them, and links will be added as we verify. Not confirmed at all: Google CPC and CPM averages (not published by Google), Tokyo Metro and Toei prices, other broadcasters' rate cards and most magazine rate cards.

6. Updated each quarter. Prices change with seasons and rate-card revisions. The date at the top shows the last check.

Limits

These are not quotes. Auction prices move daily, broadcast and outdoor prices are negotiated, and some agency-listed prices may include tax or discounts that the source does not state. Use the rate card to judge scale and to ask better questions. If you spot an error, a more recent figure or a primary source for a class D row, please tell us so it can be corrected.
