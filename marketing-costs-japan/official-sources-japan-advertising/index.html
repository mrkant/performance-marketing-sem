Where to find official information and who to contact for advertising in Japan

By Shashikant · Updated 19 November 2026 · How this hub is made and checked

⚡ In one minute

Where to find official data, transit media guides and rules, and what to bring to a first call with an agency.

This hub is an information resource. It explains how advertising is sold in Japan and what published guides say it costs. For a real quote, availability or legal advice, you need to talk to the seller or a qualified professional. These are the places to start.

Market data

Dentsu publishes the annual "Advertising Expenditures in Japan" report each March. The 2025 edition is at dentsu.co.jp (Japanese).

Train and station advertising

For JR East and several other lines, start with the media guide of JR East Marketing & Communications (jeki) at jeki.co.jp/transit_ad (Japanese). For other railways, use the operator's own advertising page.

Rules for advertisers

The Consumer Affairs Agency (消費者庁) publishes the rules and guidance on misleading representations and stealth marketing. Search for the agency's current notice on "ステルスマーケティング" for the original text.

Price guides and media sheets

Comparison sites such as Imitsu and Media Radar publish price guides and downloadable media sheets. They are useful for scale, but they are commercial comparison sites, so check how each is funded and treat their examples as indicative.

What to bring to a first call

Your goal in one sentence, the audience and area, the period, a budget range, what you already have (creative, website, tracking), and how you will judge success. With these, any agency or media owner can respond faster and more precisely.

Services outside this hub

Public relations, events, pop-up stores and fashion shows are specialist work that this hub does not cover in depth. Use a specialist agency, and ask for references and a written scope for each.

Independence note

This site does not sell advertising, take payment from agencies or receive fees for referrals. If that ever changes, it will be disclosed clearly on the affected pages.

Rate cards and published guides give scale, not a final price. Real costs move with demand, season, volume and negotiation. Use the figures on this hub and on official sites to decide whether a medium is in the right order of magnitude for your budget, then request current quotes. Treat every number as a starting point for a conversation, not as a guarantee.

How to use official and published sources

About this resource. This is an independent educational hub on shashikant.jp. It explains how advertising and marketing work in Japan using published ranges, public information and teaching examples. It is not an agency, not a consultancy, and not an offer of services. Figures are indicative and may change. For real decisions, obtain current quotes and, where appropriate, qualified local advice on contracts and compliance. Last reviewed November 2026.

When you need definitive rules on claims, premiums or labelling, go to the primary regulators rather than secondary summaries. This educational hub points to the landscape; it does not replace formal advice on a specific campaign.

Media owners and their sales agents publish rate information with varying levels of detail. Transit and television inventory, in particular, moves with demand and season. Always treat published figures as a starting point for a conversation with the current seller.

Dentsu’s annual advertising expenditure estimates are the most widely referenced market totals. They are useful for understanding the relative size of internet, television, newspapers and other categories. They are not a substitute for current rate cards or negotiated quotes.
