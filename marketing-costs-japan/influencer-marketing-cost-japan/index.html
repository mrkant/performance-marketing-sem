Influencer marketing in Japan: costs and rules

By Shashikant · Updated 19 November 2026 · How this hub is made and checked

⚡ In one minute

Direct deals are often ¥2–6 per follower per post. Sponsored posts must be clearly labelled under the stealth marketing rule in force since October 2023.

Japanese influencer marketing covers Instagram, TikTok, YouTube, X and blogs, with a strong beauty, fashion, food, travel and lifestyle focus. Companies either hire creators directly, use a matching platform or hire a casting agency.

What it costs

Direct deals are commonly priced by followers at about ¥2–6 per follower , so an account with 100,000 followers costs around ¥200,000–600,000 for a post. Matching platforms often work on ¥2–4 per follower or on performance. A single small creator may cost tens of thousands of yen, while campaigns with several creators run from hundreds of thousands to several million yen. Casting agencies add a management fee, and planning, creative and reporting add more.

How it works

You set goals and a brief, shortlist creators whose audiences match yours, agree deliverables, review drafts, publish and measure. Good agencies also secure usage rights so you can reuse the content in ads.

Watch the rules

Since October 2023, Japan's Consumer Affairs Agency treats undisclosed paid endorsements, known as stealth marketing, as improper representation. Sponsored posts must be clearly labelled, and brands are responsible for their creators' compliance. Cosmetics and health products face additional claim restrictions under the Pharmaceuticals and Medical Devices Act. Confirm current requirements with a specialist before launching.

Stealth marketing rule: verified summary

Verified Japan's Consumer Affairs Agency designated stealth marketing, meaning advertising that consumers cannot recognise as advertising, as an improper representation under Article 5, item 3 of the Act against Unjustifiable Premiums and Misleading Representations. It was announced on 28 March 2023 and took effect on 1 October 2023. Published guidance describes the rule as applying to the business that advertises its own goods or services, not to influencers or consumers, and as covering posts by third parties when the business took part in deciding the content. Businesses are expected to label such content clearly, for example as an advertisement or promotion. A breach can lead to an administrative order. Check the Consumer Affairs Agency's current guidance before running a campaign.

Before you pay: what to ask and how to measure it

Check audience match, not just follower counts. Agree posting dates, usage rights for reuse in ads, approval steps and clear advertisement labelling. Ask what the creator's past posts achieved. Track results with links or promotion codes, because likes alone do not show sales.

An influencer’s audience is lending the brand their trust. If the product or service experience fails, that trust is damaged for both parties. Brief creators honestly, ensure fulfilment and support can handle the response, and prefer long-term relationships over one-off blasts when the category benefits from repeated endorsement.

Brand and experience implications

Track both leading indicators (reach, engagement, content quality) and business indicators (unique codes, tracked links, lift in branded search, assisted conversions). Pure vanity metrics are easy to buy and hard to connect to revenue. For larger programmes, consider a structured test design rather than one-off posts.

Measurement

Japan has clear expectations around advertising disclosure. Influencer content that is effectively advertising must be identifiable as such. Ignoring disclosure risks both regulatory attention and audience trust. Build disclosure into the brief and the contract; do not treat it as optional.

Disclosure and rules

Fees vary by follower count, engagement quality, platform, content type (story, reel, YouTube integration, blog) and usage rights. Micro-influencers are often more cost-efficient for engagement and trust; larger creators cost more and deliver broader reach. Agency or platform fees may sit on top. Always clarify whether the fee includes content rights for paid amplification and for how long.
