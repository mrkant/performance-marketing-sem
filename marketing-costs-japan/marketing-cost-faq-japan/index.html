Marketing costs in Japan: frequently asked questions

By Shashikant · Updated 19 November 2026 · How this hub is made and checked

⚡ In one minute

Short answers to common questions on budgets, freelancers versus agencies, Japanese-language sites, timelines and fees.

How much should I budget to start?

For ads, plan enough budget to gather data: often ¥100,000–300,000 per month in media spend at minimum, plus fees. Websites and landing pages are one-off costs of ¥100,000–1 million depending on scope.

Is it cheaper to hire a freelancer?

Usually, yes, because there is no agency overhead. The trade-off is capacity and breadth. Many businesses start with a freelancer and move to an agency as complexity grows.

Do I need a Japanese-language website?

If you want Japanese customers, yes. Even customers who read English tend to trust local-language pages with local contact details far more.

How long until I see results?

Ads can produce leads within days, but need a couple of months to optimise. Social takes two to six months. SEO takes four to nine months or more.

Should I pay on performance only?

It lowers risk but can distort behaviour. A mix of a modest fixed fee and a performance bonus often aligns incentives better than either extreme.

Are these prices fixed?

No. They are market ranges, and scope, industry, season and provider quality all move them. Get at least three quotes and compare scope line by line.

About this guide

This guide is an independent educational resource and is not a quotation or an offer of services. It will be updated as prices and rules change; ranges were last checked in November 2026. Corrections and questions are welcome through the contact details on this site.

Sources include: Dentsu, "2025 Advertising Expenditures in Japan" (March 2026) and the CARTA HOLDINGS/Dentsu/Dentsu Digital/Septeni internet advertising analysis; published 2026 price guides from Imitsu, Meetsmore, Issoh, Atsoho, StockSun, Tatap, Pamxy, Ownly and Ecnomikata; and Japanese agency pages.

Some effects of advertising are real and still hard to attribute: brand familiarity that makes a later search more likely, a recommendation that happens offline, or a reduction in price sensitivity. Accept that a portion of value will sit outside the dashboard. Use the numbers you can trust for optimisation, and use judgement plus periodic brand research for the rest.

What cannot be measured cleanly

Cost, performance and experience are linked. A low media cost that drives traffic to a weak page or a slow service process still produces expensive customers — or no customers. When you compare quotes, look at the whole chain: media, creative, landing experience, fulfilment and support. The cheapest line item is not always the cheapest outcome.

How the numbers connect

A budget that cannot answer these questions clearly is usually not ready to be spent.

  • What is the primary job of this money — awareness, response, or both — and how will we know if that job was done?
  • What is included in the quoted fee, and what will appear later as extras?
  • Who owns the accounts, data and creative if the relationship ends?
  • Can the website, support and fulfilment handle a rise in attention without quality falling?
  • What will we stop doing if the early numbers are weak?
