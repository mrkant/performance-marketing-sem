Social media management in Japan: costs and how it works

By Shashikant · Updated 19 November 2026 · How this hub is made and checked

⚡ In one minute

Most social media management costs ¥100,000–500,000 a month with a three- to six-month minimum. Followers are not sales, so agree one business measure up front.

Social media agencies run company accounts on Instagram, X, TikTok, YouTube, Facebook and LINE. By 2026 the work has moved beyond simple posting. Short video is now a standard part of many contracts, and the better providers connect organic content with advertising and e-commerce traffic.

What it includes

Account strategy, content calendar, copywriting, graphics and video, posting, replying to comments, monthly reporting and improvement proposals. Higher tiers add shooting, influencer collaboration and paid promotion.

What it costs

The common range is ¥100,000–500,000 per month . A rough ladder: ¥50,000–150,000 for light posting support (eight to twelve posts), ¥150,000–300,000 for standard management with graphics and replies, ¥300,000–500,000 for strategy plus advertising, and ¥500,000–1 million or more for multi-channel work with filming. By platform, Instagram runs about ¥100,000–400,000, X ¥50,000–250,000, TikTok ¥150,000–500,000, LINE ¥50,000–200,000 and YouTube ¥200,000–600,000 because editing is heavy.

How it works

Providers usually require three to six months. Growth is gradual: expect the first month for setup and the first signs of traction in months two and three. Choose a provider with experience in your industry and meet the person who will actually run your account.

Before you pay: what to ask and how to measure it

Ask who will run the account, how many posts and videos are included, how comments and complaints are handled, and what the monthly report shows. Followers are not sales. Agree one business measure, such as enquiries or store visits, and check the three- to six-month minimum term.

Sources: published 2026 Japanese price guides (Imitsu, Meetsmore, Issoh, Atsoho, StockSun, Tatap, Pamxy, Ownly, Ecnomikata, NTT East). Ranges are indicative, last checked November 2026. This is an educational resource, not an offer of services.

  • Paying for volume of posts rather than quality and testing discipline.
  • Ignoring Japanese platform norms and language quality.
  • Separating organic social completely from paid social and creative production.
  • Measuring only vanity metrics while the business outcome remains unclear.

Common pitfalls

Social channels are public customer-service surfaces. Response tone, speed and accuracy affect brand perception as much as the content calendar does. Brands that treat social purely as a broadcast channel often create a gap between the promise in the content and the experience when someone actually asks a question. Align community management standards with the rest of customer service.

Brand, service and experience implications

Platform metrics (reach, engagement, video views) are easy to report and easy to game. Business metrics (traffic quality, assisted conversions, support deflection, brand search) are harder and more useful. Agree which numbers matter for decisions. A high engagement rate on content that never leads to consideration or purchase is not a success.

Measurement realities

Social media management has moved beyond scheduled posts. Short video, community response, creator collaboration, paid amplification and rapid creative testing are now core. A retainer that only covers static posts will under-serve most brands. Clarify the content mix, the expected volume of video, the response-time standards, and whether paid media management is included or separate.
