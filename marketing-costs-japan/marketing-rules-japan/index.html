Advertising rules and compliance in Japan

By Shashikant · Updated 19 November 2026 · How this hub is made and checked

⚡ In one minute

Japan restricts misleading claims, stealth marketing and the handling of personal data. Cosmetics, supplements and medicines carry extra rules, so check claims before production.

Language and localisation

Direct translation rarely works. Japanese consumers expect polite, detailed and reassuring copy, with clear information about delivery, returns, ingredients, specifications and company identity. Landing pages in Japan are often longer than Western ones for this reason. Product names, units, size charts and payment methods all need local adaptation.

Payment and delivery expectations

Convenience-store payment, credit cards, bank transfer, PayPay and other wallets, and cash on delivery all remain in use. Fast, reliable delivery with time-slot choices is standard, and slow shipping loses sales.

Advertising and labelling law

The Act against Unjustifiable Premiums and Misleading Representations restricts exaggerated claims such as "No. 1" or "best" without evidence. Cosmetics, supplements and medical-related products have specific claim limits, and stealth marketing is restricted. Specified commercial transaction rules require certain disclosures on online shops. Treat this as awareness, not legal advice, and check with a qualified professional.

Privacy

The Act on the Protection of Personal Information governs customer data, including data sent to overseas platforms and tools. Cookie and tracking practices are evolving, so consent management and server-side measurement deserve early attention.

Contracts and invoicing

Expect formal estimates, written contracts, minimum terms and monthly invoices with consumption tax (10%). Qualified invoice registration numbers matter to Japanese companies claiming tax credits, so check whether a supplier is registered. Payment is often due at month end for the following month.

Relationships

Japanese clients value responsiveness, accuracy and detailed reporting. Missed deadlines and unclear reports damage trust faster than a weak first result.

Rules and cautions

Advertising in Japan is subject to the Act against Unjustifiable Premiums and Misleading Representations, with additional rules for cosmetics, supplements, medicines, food, real estate and finance. Broadcasters and newspapers review ad copy before publication. Check claims with a qualified professional before you commit to production.

Stealth marketing rule: verified summary

Verified Japan's Consumer Affairs Agency designated stealth marketing, meaning advertising that consumers cannot recognise as advertising, as an improper representation under Article 5, item 3 of the Act against Unjustifiable Premiums and Misleading Representations. It was announced on 28 March 2023 and took effect on 1 October 2023. Published guidance describes the rule as applying to the business that advertises its own goods or services, not to influencers or consumers, and as covering posts by third parties when the business took part in deciding the content. Businesses are expected to label such content clearly, for example as an advertisement or promotion. A breach can lead to an administrative order. Check the Consumer Affairs Agency's current guidance before running a campaign.

When a campaign involves food, health, cosmetics, finance or premiums, the risk level rises. In those cases the educational material on this site is only orientation. Specific legal review is appropriate before large or sensitive spend.

The safest operating posture for most advertisers is simple: make only claims you can substantiate, disclose commercial relationships clearly (including influencer work), and treat Japanese-language accuracy as a compliance issue as well as a marketing one. Direct translations of foreign claims often create problems.
