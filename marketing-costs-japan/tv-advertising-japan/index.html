Television advertising in Japan: costs and how it works

By Shashikant · Updated 19 November 2026 · How this hub is made and checked

What it costs

Two costs apply: airtime and production.

Published range Production runs about ¥1–6 million for a standard live-action commercial, ¥500,000–3 million for animation, and over ¥10 million with well-known talent. Talent fees range from about ¥5 million up to ¥100 million. Airtime on a national key station starts at about ¥750,000 for a 15-second spot, and prime-time national spots can exceed ¥1 million each. Regional and satellite stations can be used for tests from roughly ¥300,000. Regional campaigns in areas such as Kansai are listed at ¥20–50 million for airtime, and a national campaign at ¥130 million or more including production.

How it works

There are two buying methods. Time CMs are sponsorship of a programme: they usually run for a fixed six-month period at a set budget, with a sponsor credit. Spot CMs are bought between programmes by audience rating points and run for whatever length of campaign you choose. Most advertisers buy through an agency, which negotiates schedules and handles creative approvals.

Why advertisers choose it

Interpretation Television still gives unmatched reach in a short time, and in Japan it signals scale and trust. It makes sense for mass-market products and launches with the budget to sustain it. For most small businesses, digital and regional options are more realistic first steps.

Television still confers a certain legitimacy for many audiences. That legitimacy increases the cost of a mismatch: if the ad creates a premium expectation and the website, packaging or service does not meet it, the damage is more visible. Align the creative promise with the actual customer experience before the flight runs.

Brand and experience implications

Television does not provide click-level data. Practical approaches include unique URLs or codes, search-volume and branded-search lift, geo or time-based tests, and formal brand-lift studies for larger spends. Do not judge television solely by last-click attribution from digital channels; that method systematically under-values reach media. Decide in advance what success looks like for the flight.

Measurement realities

Television remains effective for broad reach, for categories where visual demonstration helps, and for regional campaigns where local stations offer accessible entry points. It is rarely the best first medium for a pure direct-response objective with a tight CPA target. It works better when the brand can absorb attention and when other channels are ready to capture the interest television creates.

When television still fits

Television requires both airtime and production. A cheap spot in a low-attention slot with weak creative is rarely a bargain. Production quality, length and the clarity of the offer matter as much as the GRP or the time slot. Budget for both sides of the equation and for the measurement approach you will use after the flight.

Sources and evidence notes

Before you pay: what to ask and how to measure it

Ask which audience measure the plan uses, which programmes and time slots it covers, and how often the commercial will be seen. Separate airtime, production, talent and agency fees. Plan how you will track search and sales changes, since television does not click through.
