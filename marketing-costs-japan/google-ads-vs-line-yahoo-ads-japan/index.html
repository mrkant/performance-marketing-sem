Google Ads versus LINEヤフー広告 in Japan: where each one reaches

By Shashikant · Updated 19 November 2026 · How this hub is made and checked

⚡ In one minute

Yahoo! search ads were renamed LINEヤフー広告 検索広告 on 1 April 2026. Google carries most search traffic, but LINEヤフー says its services reach most of Japan, so a small test can add reach rather than repeat it.

Many foreign advertisers assume Yahoo! is a smaller copy of Google. In Japan it is a separate advertising platform with its own audience, rules and buying system, and since 2026 it is combined with LINE. This guide explains what each platform reaches and how to decide whether to use both.

What changed in 2026

Verified LINEヤフー for Business states that the former Yahoo!広告 検索広告 was renamed LINEヤフー広告 検索広告 on 1 April 2026. Search ads appear on Yahoo! JAPAN search results and are charged per click. Its display ads (運用型) combine the former Yahoo! display and LINE ad products.

Reach: what each side claims

Company-reported LINEヤフー reports about 100 million monthly active LINE users in Japan (March 2026) and about 54 million monthly logged-in Yahoo! JAPAN IDs (March 2025). It also reports that its services together reach about 80% of the population, and that 26.4% of its users use only its services. These are the company's own figures and show scale, not guaranteed ad reach.

Search share: a different picture

StatCounter's September 2026 all-device search share shows Yahoo! at about 7.6%, and about 14% on mobile in August. The two sets of figures measure different things: search traffic on one side and broad portal and app usage on the other. One industry analysis notes that Yahoo! JAPAN search results are served from Google's index, so ranking in Google already covers organic Yahoo! visibility. Ads work differently: you buy them separately on each platform.

How to test both

Interpretation Run Google for volume. Treat LINEヤフー広告 as a separate test with its own budget, tracking and target cost per order. Measure the incremental orders, not just the platform's own reports. Watch brand terms in particular: if both platforms claim the same branded searches, you may pay twice for one customer.

Questions to ask an agency

Which platform will report which conversions, and how will duplicates be removed? Will keywords, copy and landing pages be written separately for each platform? What share of budget goes to each and why? What happens to the fee if we move budget between them? Who holds the account on each platform?
