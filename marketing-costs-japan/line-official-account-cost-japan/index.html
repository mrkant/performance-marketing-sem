LINE official account setup and operation costs in Japan

By Shashikant · Updated 19 November 2026 · How this hub is made and checked

⚡ In one minute

Setup ranges from ¥100,000–300,000 with a freelancer to ¥2–5 million at a large agency, plus monthly operation and LINE's own plan fees.

LINE is used by most people in Japan. Businesses use official accounts to send messages, coupons and reservation links, and to run automated step campaigns. It is particularly strong for retail, salons, clinics, restaurants and services.

What it costs

Setup varies widely: ¥100,000–300,000 from freelancers, ¥500,000–1.5 million from specialist companies and ¥2–5 million from large agencies for complex builds with extension tools, scenario design and rich menus. Monthly operation is usually billed separately, and LINE charges its own plan fee based on the number of messages. Always check whether a quote includes the platform fee.

How it works

You design the customer journey, build the rich menu and message scenarios, create ways to collect friends (QR codes, in-store signs, website buttons), then send and measure messages. List growth and message relevance matter more than send volume, because blocked accounts are a real cost.

Before you pay: what to ask and how to measure it

Confirm whether the quote includes LINE's own plan fee, how friends will be collected, how many messages are planned each month, and who writes them. Too many messages leads to blocks. Measure friends gained, blocks, clicks and redemptions.

Sources: published 2026 Japanese price guides (Imitsu, Meetsmore, Issoh, Atsoho, StockSun, Tatap, Pamxy, Ownly, Ecnomikata, NTT East). Ranges are indicative, last checked November 2026. This is an educational resource, not an offer of services.

  • Buying a higher plan for message volume without a clear content and automation plan.
  • Using the channel mainly for one-way promotion and ignoring two-way service use cases.
  • Failing to integrate LINE events into the wider analytics and CRM picture.
  • Ignoring the rules on message frequency and consent.

Common pitfalls

A LINE account is part of the service experience. Slow or generic replies, broken reservation links, or overly promotional messaging damage trust faster than most advertising mistakes. Treat the channel as customer service infrastructure that also happens to carry marketing messages. Align tone, response times and fulfilment promises with the rest of the brand.

Brand, service and experience implications

LINE can show open rates, click rates and conversion events when properly tagged. It is stronger at nurturing existing relationships than at pure cold acquisition. Do not judge a LINE programme solely by last-click ROAS from new customers; its value often appears in repeat purchase, support deflection and lifetime value. Track both direct conversions and downstream behaviour (repeat orders, support tickets avoided, app opens) where possible.

Measurement and the limits of attribution

Plan fees cover the right to send a certain volume of messages and to use richer features. On top of that come setup, design of the rich menu, automation flows, creative for messages, and ongoing management. Many businesses underestimate the creative and operational cost of keeping the channel useful. A cheap plan with poor message quality still trains users to ignore the brand.

What the money actually buys

LINE remains the default messaging app for a large share of the Japanese population. An official account is often the most direct owned channel a brand has: it can deliver coupons, reservation links, shipping updates and automated step messages without relying on an algorithm’s goodwill. It is also a compliance-sensitive channel; message frequency and content are regulated more tightly than many social feeds.
