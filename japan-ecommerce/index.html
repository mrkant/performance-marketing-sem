Japan E-commerce: Choose a Channel and Plan Your Launch

By Shashi Kant · Strategy

Editorial guide published by Shashi Kant. Platform names identify the services discussed. Consult the linked providers for their official terms.

Which route should I test first?

Start with one channel that tests an identifiable buyer need, can fulfil the promised order and leaves positive contribution after returns and selling costs.

  • An owned store requires its own traffic and operating budget.
  • Use the marketplace cost guide for pricing after you have narrowed the route.

Which e-commerce channel should you test first in Japan?

Choose the channel that gives you the quickest credible answer about purchasing demand and contribution, within the operating capacity you already have or can build. A marketplace can provide a place to test a defined product and delivery promise. An owned store makes sense when communicating the offer, maintaining an ongoing customer relationship or controlling the shopping experience matters enough to justify bringing your own traffic. A retailer or distributor can be useful where existing demand, local service or physical access matters. Write down the question you want the first channel to answer and decide what evidence would justify a second one.

Give each channel a specific commercial job

For a small catalogue of standardized products, focus the marketplace test on findability, availability, fulfilment and an acceptable landed contribution. For a product requiring education, selection or after-sales help, make the information journey part of the test. A short discount test could change measured conversion while leaving unanswered questions about how to choose the product. Look at the questions people ask before ordering and whether support costs drop when the explanation improves.

With a distributor, decide which market intelligence you need back. Request stock, sell-through, reasons for lost sales and the forecast cadence. A distributor's order is a sale into the channel. It does not show how quickly consumers purchased or whether a second order is likely. If inventory is on consignment, the relevant cash and ownership checkpoints can differ again. Confirm the terms before modelling the acquisition cost.

Where a marketplace, direct store or retailer earns its place

Evaluate the role of each route for the same offer and delivery promise. A marketplace is attractive when buyers already search for the product or close substitutes and the seller can fulfil reliably under the rules. A direct store can support detailed product selection or service journeys, though search and conversion budgets must be funded. A retail relationship may deliver trust and product access, with its own wholesale discount, returns obligations and data limitations.

Use category economics to test route fit. A premium accessory may need a detailed compatibility guide and an easy return process, while a repeat household consumable may need honest delivery timing and a profitable replenishment cycle. A category label such as consumer goods is too broad to pick a channel. The same brand can run a marketplace replenishment range and a direct site for high-consideration items, provided it assigns inventory and promotion responsibilities clearly.

Select the merchant of record and settle responsibilities

Before making a checkout available, ask who contracts with the customer, issues receipts, owns inventory at each stage, handles complaints and bears the cost of refunds. Document exactly where title and responsibility change. Ask a qualified adviser about the entity, tax and import treatment that follows from the actual flow, especially if the seller is outside Japan. JETRO's setup materials give starting points for entity questions JETRO: setting up business in Japan.

Build a process map that starts when a shopper sees an offer and ends when the order has been settled or returned. Test the map with a first order from a distant delivery area, an out-of-stock order and a refund. Confirm who can change the promised delivery date and who tells the buyer. A process may look workable in a presentation and still fail when teams disagree about whether the warehouse or seller issues refunds.

Price the whole route and watch working capital

Start with customer receipts and reconcile discounts, taxes, refunds and settlement timing. Make variable fulfilment, item cost, delivery, advertising, payment, discounts and return costs visible. Add recurring and setup costs on a separate line. Inventory can generate a cash requirement well before it earns contribution, so add a schedule for purchase deposits, transit, receiving, sale and settlement. Check the cash tied up even in the high-demand scenario.

Use the same basis across channels and show who pays. If a retail partner deducts its incentives before remitting cash, include that cost in the waterfall only once. If your warehouse handles replacements at no additional platform charge, the work can still consume labour and stock. A business can have a positive margin per order and a cash shortage because the stock cycle and payout cycle are longer than expected.

Design a pilot you can end

Set eligibility, service levels and success thresholds before ordering stock. Use a small set of representative products and a clear Japanese buying experience. Set a budget in yen for inventory commitments, marketing, translation, shipping, returns and support. Write a separate cap for net loss and a rule for closing the pilot, including how returns and warranty requests will be handled afterward.

A pilot should leave a record that makes the next decision easier. Publish a weekly scorecard with saleable stock, paid and organic discovery, orders, cancellations, refunds, support work, unit contribution and cumulative cash committed. Call out unresolved issues. If the initial signal is weak and delivered service was also poor, the result says more about a broken test than about demand. Fix one obvious issue or stop. Avoid treating another advertising campaign as a cure for a systemic operating failure.

A channel comparison a CFO can reproduce

In this hypothetical example, a home storage brand has ¥2 million to learn whether its modular organizers can sell profitably. It has 600 saleable units and a warehouse that can guarantee 30 orders per weekday. Sales proposes starting on three platforms. Operations points out that each would need product pages, support scripts and independent return reconciliation. The decision is which experiment the team can run faithfully in 60 days, not which platform has the biggest audience.

The marketplace scenario uses a ¥4,800 net order value, ¥1,700 product cost, ¥1,150 for platform and fulfilment, ¥350 for returns and ¥650 for acquisition. Contribution is ¥950. The owned route uses ¥4,800, ¥1,700, ¥950 for payment and fulfilment, ¥350 for returns and ¥1,150 for acquisition. It contributes ¥650. The distributor offers ¥3,000 net per unit and handles most consumer fulfilment. After ¥1,700 product cost and ¥350 in delivery and support allowance, contribution is ¥950. These identical marketplace and distributor margins conceal a difference in cash timing and customer data.

Test a marketplace with an assortment small enough for one inventory system. Use twelve weeks of net margin and repeat demand to decide whether the direct store is worth a separate customer education test. If a distributor offers a binding minimum, negotiate access to sell-through and service feedback. The decision changes if the product attracts a premium for clearer compatibility instructions or if the distributor requires long return rights.

Run a catalogue readiness review in one hour

Bring the merchandising lead, warehouse lead, finance owner and Japanese reviewer together. Give each reviewer two SKUs without context and ask them to trace correct fulfilment from first impression to returns. Take the manufacturer's file, marketplace screen, reality in the carton, a proposed receipt and the support macro. An unsupported "fits all models" claim should trigger evidence review. A box with the wrong UPC requires operational correction. Both can be hidden by spending on ads that boost only visibility.

Record the output per SKU, with issue, consequence, person responsible, earliest safe launch date and proof of fix. A side table should identify tasks that can proceed while the SKU is blocked. For instance, editorial Japanese copy can sometimes be prepared alongside a revised specification, but it should not go live until matched. The launch owner needs power to make the blocking list real.

A good review also asks who pays for a correction. Existing inventory might require labels, repacking or withdrawal. If mistakes have already reached shoppers, customer support needs a response and operations may need to identify affected batches. Preserve a dated record of the before and after state. Consider regular reviews when products, claims, packaging or channel arrangements change.

When to use a marketplace to learn and your own store to serve

Imagine a specialist outdoor accessory brand. Most shoppers know the product category but do not know which sizes fit a Japanese rack standard. The business wants a marketplace range for shoppers who know the model and a direct fit guide for customers with unusual configurations. Merchandising must avoid incompatible variant names and promise the same returns support wherever buyers shop unless the terms genuinely differ.

Measure each route by its job. The marketplace needs relevant impressions, in-stock offer share, order quality and repeat purchase where available. The direct page should help users choose a fit and may earn support savings even if a visitor ultimately purchases elsewhere. Don't read a lack of last-click sales as evidence that compatibility content has no value. Collect enquiry reasons, track referrals where permitted and look at overall sales and return reasons by product.

Keep a documented decision on which channel gets stock when demand is constrained. Ranking and media in one place can be harmed by stockouts, while missed direct orders can harm expectations with existing customers. Assign a minimum stock level per route or keep a buffer you can move. Update product promises before the handoff deadline. Disagreement over stock is an operating issue to solve before adding channels.

How much should a return reserve be?

Start by separating refund value from handling. A product that is returned and can be sold again does not have the same cost as an item considered unsaleable. A refund may reverse a platform fee, or some part may remain. Delivery, packaging, inspection and reverse freight can have their own terms. Estimate expected cost per shipment from real proportions rather than spreading the highest observed loss across the entire range.

In an illustrative reserve model, 200 shipments have 10 expected returns. Six can go back into stock after ¥450 handling each, and four are unsaleable with ¥2,000 combined product and handling cost each. If reverse shipment is ¥600 on every return and is a separate cost, the expenses are ¥2,700, ¥8,000 and ¥6,000. Dividing ¥16,700 by 200 yields ¥83.50 per shipment for these lines. Revenue reversals and any unrecovered fees still need their own treatment.

Separate avoidable technical causes from commercial service choices. If two variants are hard to distinguish, a photo and chart can reduce ordering errors. Changing the warranty promise may shift the number of sales and customer support expectations. Treat interventions as separate decisions. Reserve aggressively at first, then adjust from settled cohorts and explain the change to finance.

Build your launch sequence around commitments

At the first decision meeting verify that the team can name the merchant of record and can serve the first fifteen orders in Japanese. Record product eligibility, label and packaging review, refund ownership and the forecast for stock receiving. If any are unclear, use the sprint for setup rather than marketing. List what other research may proceed in parallel. A retailer interview is cheaper to reverse than buying a pallet.

After the first sale, plan a 30-day period with only enough assortment to trace what caused a good or bad outcome. Set a contribution target with a margin floor and a separate cash cap. Rate each opportunity by confidence and cost to learn. Do not score the new platform against an established route without accounting for creative age, delivery differences and volume. Use the comparisons to choose what to investigate, not to prove a winner prematurely.

At day 60 or 90, use the launch record to choose one of three actions: expand with documented changes, extend once for a specific measurement gap, or stop and complete existing obligations. The finance role should own committed cash. The channel role should own operation and learning. A decision to stop may be a successful outcome if it prevents an uneconomic annual commitment.

Decision exercises for your team

These scenarios are hypothetical. They do not describe actual clients, sellers or campaign results. Use the questions and sample inputs to review your own evidence, terms and operating limits.

Should a premium product enter through a retailer first?

Hypothetical scenario. A homeware brand needs demonstrations because shoppers cannot easily judge the material online.

Test a few representative stores and observe the first product questions before creating large stock obligations. A wholesale order is a shipment into a retailer. The test should capture consumer sell-through and reasons interested visitors do not buy.

Prepare the review. To make this review possible, gather retailer terms, display specifications, inventory agreement, sell-through and the product language required on retail pages. Put the materials in the order the next reviewer will need them: the original evidence first, the interpretation second, and the decision third. Where two records disagree, keep both and identify who can supply the missing explanation.

For this case, advance only when the distribution partner can return enough evidence to help the next assortment decision. Identify the role that can stop the action and make sure the owner can see the evidence before the next commitment becomes irreversible.

Watch for this. Record returns rights and mark-down sharing before treating the initial order as a durable win.

Make a channel choice with a severe delivery constraint

Hypothetical scenario. An importer can dispatch only on two weekdays and has limited local customer service.

Match the proposed order and dispatch promise to the actual staffing and carrier coverage. A more demanding platform could expose an operating limit that a smaller store pilot could manage. Compare expected workload and buyer promise together.

Challenge the conclusion. Look for regional shipping rate, cutoff times, holiday roster, order mix and expected exception handling. Distinguish facts the team can observe today from assumptions it will test in the next period. Ask a reviewer outside the original implementation to challenge the strongest explanation. If the team cannot reconstruct the sequence, record the issue as unresolved.

The proposed decision rule is to approve a route with a controlled order ceiling until service performance is known. Record the earliest date the owner can make that decision and what activity should pause until the conditions are met.

Watch for this. A change to courier terms can justify revisiting the original decision.

Model overlap between wholesale and direct sales

Hypothetical scenario. An established retailer partner sells the brand and worries that a direct coupon will undercut its regular price.

Map which products, buyers and circumstances overlap and discuss the offer and timing with the appropriate team. A direct uplift could coincide with fewer retailer reorders. A staged pilot should observe portfolio effects, including relationships, inventory and service economics.

Keep the work reproducible. Use promotion plan, channel terms, relevant price observations, baseline partner demand and margin by SKU. Record which treatment applies, what changed during the observation and what part of the business was outside the scope. A dated snapshot lets another person compare the same conditions after the process or platform changes.

At the review, run a limited offer with a pre-agreed comparison and exit condition. Give the next owner a brief note on the decision, the facts that support it and one finding that would warrant reopening it.

Watch for this. Review commercial terms and competition-law questions with advisers where appropriate.

Build a market-entry memo

Hypothetical scenario. An executive wants a proposal that can be approved from one meeting and revisited after the first orders.

Show the demand hypothesis, execution owners, sales and loss limits, evidence gaps and deadlines. A named owner and a dated gate are more useful than a list of ambitious growth tactics. Make explicit what the team can stop without breaking a customer promise.

Open the review with evidence. Ask the responsible team to bring customer evidence, route economics, service plan, returns workflow and the planned decision date. Compare the record with what the buyer or operator actually experienced. Keep the original terms alongside any model so a new reviewer can distinguish differences in scope from errors in arithmetic or reporting.

For this case, fund only the first reversible milestone, with the next budget tied to evidence. Identify the role that can stop the action and make sure the owner can see the evidence before the next commitment becomes irreversible.

Watch for this. The team's capability at launch determines the scope of the experiment.

Where the Japan e-commerce guides fit

The series follows three roles. A platform guide helps a merchant evaluate and operate one route. A market evidence guide explains what the public data measures and how to form a forecast. An operations guide supports decisions that recur regardless of platform, such as hiring support and preparing for specialist review. Follow the path that matches the decision you need to make next. Cost research in the existing Marketing Costs Japan hub is particularly useful once the business needs a budget for media or marketplace work. Keep that budget separate from the channel's viability as a means of reaching and serving buyers.

What to present at the next investment review

Bring four artifacts. In the offer brief, identify the buyer, the problem, the Japanese description, delivered price and distinct reasons to buy. In the delivery plan, identify the warehouse route, shipment constraints and return address. In the finance model, show unit economics, time to settle and the lowest result the business can accept. In the research brief, show the buyer evidence, the sales opportunity still unproven and the cost and date of the next test.

Include a named owner for each. The company that funds the test should know when it can stop without breaking a promise. If the result of a supplier review can block the pilot, make that visible so the team does not already commit to a store plan. Agree whether pilot results will be reviewed at the SKU, seller or brand level. Confirm that a strong result on one low-cost line will not be used to promote a much larger, riskier assortment by default.

Frequently asked questions about selling online in Japan

Can a successful marketplace pilot justify opening an owned site? Yes, if it answered a question relevant to the new site. Evidence of price readiness or demand for a product type can travel. The platform's audience access, review ecosystem and checkout experience do not travel with it. For the owned site, budget for traffic, payments and site operations separately, and test its recovery of the same customer need.

When should I revisit the channel decision? Reopen it when you learn something that changes the original economics or operating limits. Examples include repeated service failures, a fee change, a consistently better offer match, a revised return pattern or material working capital demands. Seasonal revenue swings alone are an incomplete signal. Tie each review back to the forum that approved the test and the decision it was intended to answer.
