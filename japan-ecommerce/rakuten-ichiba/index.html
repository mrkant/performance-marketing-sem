Selling on Rakuten Ichiba

By Shashi Kant · Selling channels

Editorial guide published by Shashi Kant. Platform names identify the services discussed. Consult the linked providers for their official terms.

How should I choose a Rakuten Ichiba plan?

Compare the whole contract cost under your own sales mix, including billing timing, system fees, payments and seller-funded promotions.

  • Check the public fee table and terms that apply to your store separately.
  • Compare ordinary weeks and comparable promotion periods before scaling.

Is a Rakuten shop the right launch format?

Choose Rakuten when your team can operate a branded storefront, maintain Japanese merchandising and measure profitability during and between campaigns. A store gives the operator opportunities to explain product fit, organise ranges and offer sets. It also creates ongoing work. Ask who will update store pages, check promotional terms, own order exceptions and run support if the channel succeeds. A business with one undifferentiated item should first show why store merchandising changes the purchase decision.

Translate headline plan fees into a monthly decision

Rakuten's public merchant FAQ lists monthly base fees, excluding tax, of ¥25,000 for Ganbare, ¥65,000 for Standard and ¥130,000 for Mega Shop, and a common ¥60,000 registration fee. The published plan table also distinguishes billing schedules and system fee ranges. Confirm the contract period, invoice timing and applicable device or sale bands and add payment, point, affiliate and programme costs. The plan choice with the lowest listed monthly fee is not always the plan with the lowest all-in cost for your sales mix Rakuten Ichiba: merchant plans and fees Rakuten Ichiba: merchant FAQ.

Imagine ¥900,000 in monthly sales and a two-percentage-point reduction in variable costs after upgrading a plan. The savings are ¥18,000, short of the ¥40,000 fixed difference between the first two published base fees. At ¥2 million the illustrative saving is ¥40,000. At ¥3 million it is ¥60,000. This is a sensitivity exercise, not Rakuten's fees calculator or a recommendation to upgrade. Actual rates, payment patterns, product limits, billing and contract constraints can change the outcome.

Build a Japanese storefront around a shopping decision

Plan landing paths for the new customer comparing options, the buyer who already knows the item and the returning customer trying to reorder. A brand selling storage products might need a size guide at store level and individual product pages with dimensions, installation limits and a comparison table. Give each page a useful role in the decision. Use clear titles, truthful images and mobile ordering steps. Put dates and conditions next to time-limited offers.

Require a real owner for each high-traffic area. A campaign banner can accidentally continue showing yesterday's shipping promise even after the product page is corrected. Maintain a review checklist that records the offer, pages affected, owner, expiry and rollback instruction. Check the Japanese copy for what the customer pays and what happens if a coupon is unavailable. Keep read access to the original assets and editable files.

Separate the campaign's economics from ordinary weeks

Choose a normal-week baseline that is comparable for stock, days of the week, price and delivery. Record how much of the promotion you fund and how much the platform funds, what quantities qualify and the total discount cap. An agency's campaign report may label platform attributed sales as growth. Ask for the cash settlement, returns and the next non-campaign week. A campaign can borrow demand from tomorrow or shift it from your owned site.

A simple example: 400 baseline orders contribute ¥900 each before fixed shop costs, or ¥360,000. A promotion yields 660 orders with only ¥540 contribution each, or ¥356,400. Orders grew 65%, yet channel contribution fell ¥3,600 before any extra labour. If 80 of the campaign orders later return, and reverse logistics is materially higher, the loss can widen. Decide before the campaign whether acquisition, stock reduction or short-term contribution is the goal. Each deserves a different success rule.

Reserve inventory and staff for promotions

Start with a realistic warehouse capacity number. A daily forecast of 320 orders does not fit a team that can pick, pack and hand off only 250 in the permitted carrier window. Plan a quantity cap, adjust fulfilment promise truthfully and assign authority to pause the promotion. Coordinate front-end merchandising and stock allocation so products do not keep featuring after hitting their operational ceiling.

Bundle planning requires looking at components. If a three-piece gift set consumes one unit of the top individual item, record whether selling the gift set would deprive regular customers of a profitable option. Measure stock at the component level and confirm that multi-channel inventory synchronization represents reserved stock. Run one test cancellation and a returned set. Know whether components can be resold and who updates the listing.

Grow repeat demand with permission and evidence

Rakuten's plan materials describe communication and store-management tools. Review what is available to your account and what the rules permit before asking an agency to export customer details or send promotions Rakuten Ichiba: merchant plans and fees. Provide practical reasons to return, such as replacement parts, replenishment guidance or a clear explanation of how accessories fit. Measure repeat performance within the permitted reporting environment. An email address supplied for order delivery does not give blanket permission to use the address for other marketing.

Compare first orders with reorders under similar campaign exposure. Track shifts between sale events so you can see whether shoppers are becoming dependent on a single discount cadence. A customer who repeats once after 60 days may be more valuable than a buyer who places three deeply discounted orders in one event and never returns. Define customer and order cohorts before launching the analysis.

The operating sequence for a 90-day Rakuten pilot

During setup, compare the signed plan with the public rate card, model the cautious case and get an owner for product content, finance and customer support. Use a short assortment that you can explain clearly. Have Japanese reviewers navigate from the collection to the final order on mobile. Check that points and delivery statements describe the offer consistently.

In the middle month run one bounded promotion with a clear purpose. Give the warehouse advance notice and keep a separate log of seller-funded points, fees, refunds and support labour. Compare planned and actual costs as soon as settlements are available. When figures drift, identify whether the discrepancy came from volume, the fee multiplier, basket mix, refund timing or agency work.

In the final month review an ordinary trading period. Ask which categories sell without extra subsidy and which require the campaign just to cover stock and labour. Continue only if the operating model can be repeated and the team can fund inventory for the next peak. A successful pilot should leave behind a working store, a documented calendar and a set of contribution records someone else can audit.

Decision exercises for your team

These scenarios are hypothetical. They do not describe actual clients, sellers or campaign results. Use the questions and sample inputs to review your own evidence, terms and operating limits.

Choose a plan when sales are seasonal

Hypothetical scenario. A gift company expects two peaks and a substantial gap in quiet months.

Calculate the full contracted cost and invoice calendar for both plans against a monthly forecast. A projected annual average may hide cash demands before high-season inventory arrives. Efficiency depends on unit margins and cash dates, not only fixed rates.

Prepare the review. To make this review possible, gather plan rate card, tax basis, signed terms, expected item mix and campaign participation costs. Put the materials in the order the next reviewer will need them: the original evidence first, the interpretation second, and the decision third. Where two records disagree, keep both and identify who can supply the missing explanation.

For this case, choose the plan that handles the low forecast and avoids a commitment the team cannot fund. Identify the role that can stop the action and make sure the owner can see the evidence before the next commitment becomes irreversible.

Watch for this. Ask how changing plans works before assuming you can switch after a single peak.

Audit the store between campaign dates

Hypothetical scenario. A merchant’s shop looks strong in the big event but shoppers struggle to find a basic replenishment item afterward.

Test shopping by product need from ordinary category results and from the homepage without a campaign landing link. If visitors rely on the temporary event entry, everyday navigation may be broken. Improve category labels and product fit explanations before paying to bring more ordinary shoppers.

Challenge the conclusion. Look for a clean mobile session, collection logic, stock status and a summary of customer enquiries. Distinguish facts the team can observe today from assumptions it will test in the next period. Ask a reviewer outside the original implementation to challenge the strongest explanation. If the team cannot reconstruct the sequence, record the issue as unresolved.

The proposed decision rule is to run a non-event baseline after fixing the path. Record the earliest date the owner can make that decision and what activity should pause until the conditions are met.

Watch for this. Preserve representative screenshots so the result can be checked when a new promotion changes the layout again.

Compare seller points with a coupon

Hypothetical scenario. The store can raise its own point contribution or offer a cart coupon with a limited budget.

Price each offer against the same customer price, fee base, campaign eligibility and order volume assumption. One approach may appear cheaper at equal volume while concentrating discounts on buyers who would have converted anyway. Compare incremental contribution and record any eligibility or reward approval issues.

Keep the work reproducible. Use a written funding split, coupon limits, redemption records and a sample of settled orders. Record which treatment applies, what changed during the observation and what part of the business was outside the scope. A dated snapshot lets another person compare the same conditions after the process or platform changes.

At the review, launch only one sufficiently isolated test, unless the goal is deliberately combined promotion performance. Give the next owner a brief note on the decision, the facts that support it and one finding that would warrant reopening it.

Watch for this. Keep platform-funded benefit separate from the part the seller ultimately pays.

Check inventory behind a themed collection

Hypothetical scenario. A store plans to feature a gift bundle using two components shared with ordinary listings.

Identify which component becomes scarce first and allocate the latest stock count between both offers. The chance of disappointed customers rises when the shop fixes only the public stock number. A shared item can become a service incident across all its parent offers.

Open the review with evidence. Ask the responsible team to bring opening stock by component, committed open orders, bundle bills of materials and fulfilment capacity by day. Compare the record with what the buyer or operator actually experienced. Keep the original terms alongside any model so a new reviewer can distinguish differences in scope from errors in arithmetic or reporting.

For this case, release the promotion with a lower quantity and specify the automated or manual pause trigger. Identify the role that can stop the action and make sure the owner can see the evidence before the next commitment becomes irreversible.

Watch for this. Warehouse counts are a better starting point than the number displayed in a creative brief.

Decide whether to outsource local merchandising

Hypothetical scenario. A brand can translate a standard template or hire a Japanese merchant operator who can also handle store changes.

Compare the workflow from request through approval and verify how each provider handles the same product change. A translated page may be accurate in isolation yet still fail to show how two similar items differ. The task should be assessed against a buyer decision and the intended fulfilment.

Prepare the review. To make this review possible, gather an editable page sample, approved terminology, local support availability and a rollback demonstration. Put the materials in the order the next reviewer will need them: the original evidence first, the interpretation second, and the decision third. Where two records disagree, keep both and identify who can supply the missing explanation.

The proposed decision rule is to approve a small paid trial with acceptance criteria before a large recurring commitment. Record the earliest date the owner can make that decision and what activity should pause until the conditions are met.

Watch for this. Preserve access to editable text, product photographs and component descriptions after handover.

Build a reordering experience

Hypothetical scenario. A household brand wants returning Rakuten shoppers to find replacements without relying on points.

Check whether the refill is available, clearly distinguished from the starter kit and correctly paired with compatible variants. The channel can benefit from repeat buying without needing broad data exports. Tracking within available merchant tools should respect rules and transmission limits.

Challenge the conclusion. Look for product history, recurring support questions, repeat links allowed by the platform and fulfilment outcomes. Distinguish facts the team can observe today from assumptions it will test in the next period. Ask a reviewer outside the original implementation to challenge the strongest explanation. If the team cannot reconstruct the sequence, record the issue as unresolved.

At the review, prioritize refill selection and delivery reliability before adding a more expensive loyalty campaign. Give the next owner a brief note on the decision, the facts that support it and one finding that would warrant reopening it.

Watch for this. A campaign-driven reorder does not necessarily show profitable habitual purchasing.

Reconcile the first merchant invoice

Hypothetical scenario. The store's forecast rate and the first period's actual invoice differ enough to affect contribution.

Match every fixed, payment, systems, points, affiliate and refund line to the ledger and its underlying denominator. Fixed and variable deductions can be different from the original budget simply because product and device mix differ. Trace the largest residual to a named cause.

Keep the work reproducible. Use invoice labels, exact plan, tax basis, normal and event order cohorts and account notice dates. Record which treatment applies, what changed during the observation and what part of the business was outside the scope. A dated snapshot lets another person compare the same conditions after the process or platform changes.

For this case, hold the next promotional commitment until finance can reconcile the variance. Identify the role that can stop the action and make sure the owner can see the evidence before the next commitment becomes irreversible.

Watch for this. Use the actual bill for a merchant-specific update, without publishing a private charge as a general offer.

Build a promotion P&L that includes the cash calendar

Start with units actually fulfilled, then reconcile discount and point funding. Use average order value because the promotion can shift the mix into a more expensive bundle or a smaller entry product. Deduct variable platform fees, payment, delivery and labour on compatible bases. Add a provision for excess returning stock and seller-funded creative. Model fixed fees for the plan period and record when invoices, supplier payments and settlements occur.

Here is an illustrative promotion that sells 700 orders of ¥4,500 net each. The mix requires ¥1,800 in product cost, ¥850 in fulfilment and fees, ¥250 for seller-funded points that have not already reduced the net revenue, ¥350 for marketing, and ¥200 for expected returns per order. The result is ¥1,050 per order, or ¥735,000 before fixed costs and out-of-scope overhead. If a comparable non-event cohort of 500 orders contributes ¥1,400 per order, the hypothetical baseline contribution is ¥700,000. The difference of ¥35,000 is small relative to the volume increase. It may be explained by seasonality or additional labour and should not be labelled causal.

Compare cash dates separately. A business can commit supplier deposits and campaign production before customer cash is available. Plan for a delay in settlement or a larger-than-expected return provision. Agree who can decline a second event to preserve the cash required for existing obligations. A line-level budget owner should be identified before the store accepts participation terms.

Organize category pages around fit, value and fulfilment

A storefront for kitchen appliances can create navigation around device capacity, counter size and maintenance, because those questions determine which item fits. A category page that uses only campaign names relies on the buyer already knowing the assortment. Study questions sent to support and the items customers choose after asking. Translate recurring questions into persistent guidance. Identify which changes should appear at store level and which details belong on a product SKU.

Check content under slow or one-handed mobile use. Can a visitor find the delivered price and the needed accessory without opening a series of images? Is the size shown in text that can be read by assistive technology? Make sure the team can update and approve the same information in Japanese. Visual consistency helps only when the variants and information are correct. Keep key data in editable HTML, add alt text where appropriate and verify screenshots do not contain private information.

Establish practical incentives for the operating team

An agency may have incentives to generate campaign volume while an internal team is measured on repeat demand, returns or gross margin. Agree on a common scorecard. Give the store operator credit for routine fixes to misleading information, fulfilment errors and refunds, even if those changes do not immediately grow traffic. Have finance certify each trading period's contribution using settlement cases it can reproduce.

Agree who can commit time and resources to the next calendar. Site staff, customer support, stock teams and warehouse leads should see event deadlines early. When a partner proposes a promoted item, ask whether enough stock is available and what is true about its delivery promise. Campaign confidence improves when the team can explain why ordinary operations still work outside the event.

Frequently asked questions about Rakuten Ichiba

Should I choose a plan solely by the month's forecast? Review the signed term, payments, product limits, actual fee base and the difficulty of making a plan change mid-cycle. Test scenarios over a full contract period. A large sale month can hide cash or margin problems in the quieter ones.

Does funding more points always improve unit economics? No. The effect depends on orders gained and how much is spent on orders that would already have happened. Compare net outcomes using matured cohorts and identify who funds each offer.

Check which assumptions belong to your own store

A public fee schedule allows a merchant to reproduce the rate shown for an applicable plan and category. It cannot tell the merchant how mobile basket mix, average delivery cost and returns will change when a campaign brings shoppers from a new segment. Ask finance to review the first five settlement examples after the event, including a fixed charge, a refund and a mixed-product basket. Trace the figures beyond the seller dashboard. The point of this check is to adjust the decision about the next promotion, not just to match a bank amount at period close.

When the team revises a forecast, retain the original assumptions and explain the variance. Compare a product whose fees match the forecast with one whose calculations differ sharply. If an account rule changed mid-month, annotate when it applies. A reliable record separates invoice changes from errors in choosing the cost basis or rounding.
