Temu, SHEIN, AliExpress, Alibaba.com and 1688 in Japan

By Shashi Kant · Selling channels

Editorial guide published by Shashi Kant. Platform names identify the services discussed. Consult the linked providers for their official terms.

What is the difference between using these platforms to source and to sell?

Research consumer offers, supplier procurement and eligibility to sell as separate activities. Each needs different evidence.

  • Supplier and product approval depend on the actual sample, production terms and intended use.
  • Global visits are not a measure of Japan-specific sales or merchant eligibility.

What decision are you making about Chinese-origin platforms?

Separate three possible roles. You may be researching what Japanese consumers see on a low-price shopping app, comparing suppliers, or assessing whether your business can sell on a particular platform. Access as a consumer establishes little about seller eligibility. A wholesale portal can help with supplier discovery without becoming your Japanese sales channel. Use a different evidence checklist for each role.

Temu, SHEIN and AliExpress can be investigated as consumer-facing services where their offers are available. Alibaba.com and 1688 belong in sourcing research, with different intended markets and operational expectations. Check the provider's description and the actual checkout or seller registration environment for the relevant country. Compare offers as a shopper in Japan

For a sample of similar products, record the date, delivery location, size or specification, price after applicable shipping, expected delivery, return address and warranty information. Identify whether the offer is a first-order promotion or requires a minimum basket. Compare two or three periods, because a single landing page may reflect personalisation, stock or campaign exposure. Verify the details without collecting other shoppers' information.

Avoid concluding that a domestic seller must match the lowest sticker price. Investigate what the customer gives up or gains at the delivered level. A transparent return process, complete local instructions and shorter delivery can support a different offer. Confirm through interviews or a small purchase-intent test whether those advantages matter to the target segment rather than assuming all shoppers have the same priorities.

Build a sourcing brief with enforceable acceptance conditions

Write a specification before asking for prices. Include dimensions, materials, composition where relevant, colours, testing requirements, packaging, warning text, traceability and how you will approve changes. Define which party pays for testing, repacking or replacements if the production lot differs. A sample proves only what arrived as a sample. Compare a pilot lot with the signed specification, and store the results by batch.

Source quotes often change depending on whether freight, insurance, documentation or import charges are included. Name the trade terms and verify the responsibilities you are accepting with a broker. Keep these quotes and consumer prices on separate sheets. A postal purchase for personal use does not establish the compliance route for stock a business imports for resale Japan Customs: procedures for importation Japan Customs: personal import FAQ.

Calculate landed cost on saleable units

Suppose 500 promotional items cost ¥650 each. Freight, broker and inspection total a further ¥155,000, so the initial cost is ¥480,000. If 25 units fail inspection and cannot be sold, the effective cost of the 475 saleable units is roughly ¥1,011 each. That is before domestic fulfilment, refund provision, customer service and ad spend. A quote of ¥650 is therefore only one input to the calculation.

Compare a second supplier with a higher unit quote and reliably lower rejection and freight exposure. Ask whether a business can afford the downside, including compliance checks, quarantine, disposal, reshipping and extreme shipment delay. If the order is denominated in a foreign currency, pair each scenario with its actual payment timing. Work out who approves the re-order when exchange rates or transit spend moves outside the range.

Verify seller access before making commitments

Find the official country-specific application route and confirm business type, permitted categories, payout settings, merchant of record and dispatch rules. Upload documents only to authorised channels. Determine which product and advertising claims the seller owns and which claims the platform may display. Ask how an account can end participation, retrieve stock and close open customer issues. Do not reuse a date from a launch announcement to prove eligibility for a new applicant today.

Budget a test that fits both commercial and operational goals. Select an assortment where brand rights and product obligations are clear. Set an order cap, returns address, customer response coverage and contribution floor. End the test if you cannot prove supply traceability or if the buyer receives a promise you cannot meet. A sales opportunity can still be unsuitable because seller control, data or return obligations are inadequate.

Separate competitive intelligence from legal and safety review

Use a sourcing shortlist to estimate commercial options, then have a separate product approval gate. Determine the product category, origin documentation, importer responsibilities and whether local markings, testing or approvals apply. A platform listing and a supplier assurance are evidence about an offer, not proof that your product can be lawfully sold in Japan. Record who approved the intended use and preserve the documents for review.

If a batch is accepted for a pilot, keep version control on instructions and packaging. Tie each customer order to a traceable lot where appropriate, and plan how customers would be informed if a safety concern surfaces. If the team cannot isolate the batch or identify the relevant supplier documentation, stop scaling until it can. Treat an attractive first order as an opportunity to learn, not a waiver of the operating controls.

Decision exercises for your team

These scenarios are hypothetical. They do not describe actual clients, sellers or campaign results. Use the questions and sample inputs to review your own evidence, terms and operating limits.

Observe a discounted basket

Hypothetical scenario. An executive sees a deeply discounted app offer and wants to match it on the company store immediately.

Compare the delivered price for the same specification and availability in a normal and a promotional visit. A single coupon can apply only to certain buyers or be funded by someone other than the seller. Matching it permanently can destroy contribution without proving that the same customer was lost.

Prepare the review. To make this review possible, gather dated basket observations, shipping, minimum quantity, offer conditions and an accurate specification comparison. Put the materials in the order the next reviewer will need them: the original evidence first, the interpretation second, and the decision third. Where two records disagree, keep both and identify who can supply the missing explanation.

For this case, commission a small delivered-offer study before making a permanent price change. Identify the role that can stop the action and make sure the owner can see the evidence before the next commitment becomes irreversible.

Watch for this. Capture no customer identities in competitor screenshots.

Choose a procurement route for a trial carton

Hypothetical scenario. A domestic supplier can deliver quickly, while a wholesale portal advertises a lower price with a high minimum order.

Normalise each quote to inspected saleable units at the required destination and date. A large minimum and uncertain rejects can make apparently inexpensive stock expensive. Paying more for a reversible first batch may save more than a permanent unit discount.

Challenge the conclusion. Look for supplier identity, sample specifications, inspection scope, order minimum, logistics quote and remedy for non-conforming goods. Distinguish facts the team can observe today from assumptions it will test in the next period. Ask a reviewer outside the original implementation to challenge the strongest explanation. If the team cannot reconstruct the sequence, record the issue as unresolved.

The proposed decision rule is to place a small pilot only after defining inspection and an agreed remedy. Record the earliest date the owner can make that decision and what activity should pause until the conditions are met.

Watch for this. Supplier discovery does not validate the product classification or rights to sell it.

Verify the Japanese seller channel independently

Hypothetical scenario. The team has a link to a seller programme that an agency says is open in Japan.

Locate the country-specific application and ask the platform to confirm the model and product eligibility. A consumer-facing site and another region's merchant announcement may both be genuine but fail to establish current access. Review contract and account evidence before funding listings.

Keep the work reproducible. Use current registration route, legal entity requirements, payout details, product restrictions and responsibility for dispatch and returns. Record which treatment applies, what changed during the observation and what part of the business was outside the scope. A dated snapshot lets another person compare the same conditions after the process or platform changes.

At the review, approve the route only after your own business qualifies and understands the terms. Give the next owner a brief note on the decision, the facts that support it and one finding that would warrant reopening it.

Watch for this. Avoid supplying documents through an address without independent verification.

Handle a manufacturing specification change

Hypothetical scenario. A supplier proposes replacing a plastic insert in a previously approved product.

Block automatic acceptance and ask what the change means for safety, test evidence, packaging and traceability. A small material substitution can matter if assumptions in a laboratory test depended on the old component. The issue should be resolved before a production lot is dispatched.

Open the review with evidence. Ask the responsible team to bring signed part specification, old and new samples, lot records, claims register and adviser comments. Compare the record with what the buyer or operator actually experienced. Keep the original terms alongside any model so a new reviewer can distinguish differences in scope from errors in arithmetic or reporting.

For this case, require revised approval and record where the new batch begins. Identify the role that can stop the action and make sure the owner can see the evidence before the next commitment becomes irreversible.

Watch for this. A friendly email agreeing to the colour does not establish acceptance of a different material.

Compare fulfilment risk across countries

Hypothetical scenario. The seller can dispatch to Japan directly or receive stock in a domestic warehouse first.

Map delivery promises, reverse shipment, inventory control, customs roles and remedy for a broken delivery. Direct shipment may preserve optionality on stock quantities but make returns harder. Domestic placement adds working capital and can change commercial and tax responsibilities.

Prepare the review. To make this review possible, gather shipment terms, broker plan, customer return instructions, warehouse expense and realistic delivery ranges. Put the materials in the order the next reviewer will need them: the original evidence first, the interpretation second, and the decision third. Where two records disagree, keep both and identify who can supply the missing explanation.

The proposed decision rule is to choose a trial with the service commitment and cash exposure your organisation can support. Record the earliest date the owner can make that decision and what activity should pause until the conditions are met.

Build a correction or recall drill

Hypothetical scenario. A buyer reports a safety issue that could relate to a batch from a new supplier.

Limit new exposure and preserve the product evidence while the responsible specialists evaluate next steps. The team needs to know if it can identify units, routes and dates before scaling. Contact and remedy decisions may require immediate qualified advice.

Challenge the conclusion. Look for lot number, supplier documentation, stored listing versions, orders subject to authorised access and incident roles. Distinguish facts the team can observe today from assumptions it will test in the next period. Ask a reviewer outside the original implementation to challenge the strongest explanation. If the team cannot reconstruct the sequence, record the issue as unresolved.

At the review, ensure the team can isolate an affected lot from one report before releasing the next production batch. Give the next owner a brief note on the decision, the facts that support it and one finding that would warrant reopening it.

Watch for this. Keep incident information private and share only with appropriate roles.

Choose a competitive response grounded in customer benefit

Hypothetical scenario. The marketing team proposes copying a discount, though support enquiries suggest local instructions are the bigger weakness.

Test clearer Japanese setup instructions and a transparent return path at the same delivered price. Running a price reduction and content change at once will not reveal which solved the problem. Investigate buyer objections that are commercially actionable.

Run a supplier comparison without losing control of the specification

Create a request document with version, intended use, packaging and inspection. Require the supplier to confirm what it can offer against each line and identify deviations. Ask whether a sample and pilot quantity are made in the same process and how production changes are authorized. A well-priced offer can be misleading if one quote is for thicker material or stronger packaging and another is for a lower specification. Price comparisons work only after requirements are consistent.

Evaluate inspection and delivery risk separately. A factory inspection can establish whether a random sample matched certain criteria on a particular day. It cannot validate every item in a production run. An observed shipment time likewise cannot establish certainty for the next lot. Build the accepted variability and dispute process into your purchase terms and keep alternatives available for higher-risk components.

For larger commitments, use a small release with checks at each gate. Review drawing, sample, pilot lot, package and settlement. Assign an owner to be present when a critical decision is made and record what evidence they see. If needed, have an appropriate Japanese reviewer inspect instructions and regulated wording. Consider inventory samples and supplier records as auditable material, not as decorative appendices to a procurement P&L.

Calculate the economics of replacing a defective batch

Assume a landed batch of 400 items costs ¥320,000, of which 16 fail safety or performance checks and 24 need repackaging at ¥200. Allow another ¥12,000 for inspection. If the failed items produce no recovery and the 24 repackaged items are among the 384 saleable units, divide ¥336,800 by those 384 units. The effective unit cost is about ¥877.08. Add distribution, reserves and acquisition costs before comparing alternative suppliers.

If normal demand is only 250 units per cycle, a larger wholesale minimum will extend inventory exposure even if the spreadsheet produces a lower effective cost per unit. Model the cost of reserving cash and holding safety stock. Identify whether the seller can actually restock the best-selling item at the new price and whether quality proof carries through to repeat orders. A lower first quote can lose its advantage when the original supplier cannot reproduce the specification.

Compare app discovery with sustainable seller demand

Buyer demand on a platform may depend on recommendation placement, campaign availability or first-order discounts. Measure buyer interest for the intended use case and advertised price rather than treating a strong global brand or download figure as demand for your store. Ask which order flow and merchant tools confirm Japanese sales and what happens when a promotion ends. Record whether there is an ongoing way to learn from service issues without breaching account terms or unnecessarily processing personal information.

A legitimate channel pilot asks what the seller controls before investing in translation, inventory and promotion. Identify which party sets price, allows a discount or paid placement, bears delivery obligations and approves changes. Seek clear written confirmation of any business-facing programme terms. When the role is constrained, consider a different offer that needs fewer changes, or choose another route whose responsibilities fit your team.

Frequently asked questions about the platforms

Is Alibaba.com the same operating route as 1688? No. Review how each service describes its role and test the supplier transaction you would actually enter. Checkout, documentation, protections and export support differ by supplier and model Alibaba Group: businesses and platforms.

Can I establish a platform's adoption in Japan using reported global visits? Global audience estimates do not establish Japan-specific buyer counts, Japan storefront conversion or demand for your items. Define geography, period and whether the unit is visits, shoppers or paying customers before drawing a conclusion.

Should a Japanese merchant sign up because it already shops on the platform? Verify the merchant programme in the intended country, the permitted entity and products and who handles returns. A consumer account answers a different question. Avoid using another market's notice to confirm local eligibility.

Prepare a short procurement decision brief

For each shortlisted route, state the intended role and merchandise, counterparty, currency, delivered terms, product and returns responsibilities, and the smallest batch that can be meaningfully inspected. Include examples of the information available to a reviewer and the gap most likely to block shipment. Where an agent helps with translation or ordering, record exactly which entity is submitting documents and which contract governs the transaction. An interpreter can facilitate a conversation without being the seller or liable for the goods. Confirm the contract.

Plan a threshold for disagreement that does not leave the business exposed to a full order. For example, the first pilot lot may proceed only after the users can compare actual package and contents against essential specifications. If a supplier cannot provide batch labels or approved artwork, document whether that is a correctable documentation gap or a deeper inability to provide traceability. Escalate regulated matters before deciding that a delay can be absorbed.

Recognize when tools answer a different question

A sourcing directory can help determine whether candidate suppliers exist. A consumer offer can help identify what the delivered experience looks like. A seller portal can define what an eligible merchant must do. Reusing evidence from one desk to make another decision creates avoidable blind spots. Even if a product looks identical on a consumer site, review its actual materials, markings and source. Retain test evidence for the item you plan to ship, not a visually similar product from another seller.

Keep imported stock distinct from competitor observations

Use separate records for the sample a shopper can order, the unit a supplier offers and the product your warehouse will sell. A listing photograph helps identify an offer for research. It does not demonstrate that the packaging, composition or warranty match your procurement plan. Label evidence with the intended use and review date. At the next supplier meeting, ask the team to identify which items it can approve using the documents it actually holds, and which items remain commercial hypotheses. Preserve enough detail for a buyer or reviewer to explain the differences without accessing a private platform account.
