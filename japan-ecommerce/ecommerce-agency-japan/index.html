Choosing an E-commerce Agency in Japan

By Shashi Kant · Operations and partners

Editorial guide published by Shashi Kant. Platform names identify the services discussed. Consult the linked providers for their official terms.

What should I check before hiring a marketplace agency?

Define the work and approval roles, compare quotes on the same basis, and ask for a work sample that shows how the agency diagnoses a specific issue.

  • Confirm who can access each account and how files and responsibilities transfer.
  • Ask the candidate to distinguish attributable sales, reconciliation and incremental results.

How should a business choose an e-commerce agency in Japan?

Hire against a written operating problem. Define the channel, tasks, deliverables, people, decisions, deliverable acceptance criteria and terms for returning access. A team capable of optimizing ads may not offer catalog operations, customer support, returns or settlement reconciliation. Make the distinction before extra work turns into recurring change orders.

Break the scope into outcomes you can inspect

Separate strategy, translation, store development, catalog operations, advertising, promotional planning, customer support and finance. For each, name who performs the work, approves it, pays for it and corrects errors. Define a realistic baseline and what the agency must deliver in the first month. A clear handover spreadsheet, correct tracking map and rejection log can matter more than a confident revenue forecast before the account has been audited.

Organize deliverables around real working files. A listing programme might require a SKU-by-SKU source file, Japanese description, valid identifiers, rights documentation, before-and-after screenshots, a change record and a monthly backlog. A media programme might require a search query review, campaign access, a budget allocation model and reconciliation to actual orders. Ask which work can be exported without paying a new licence.

Compare quotes using the same exposure

Build a comparison sheet that captures management fee, minimum, media, production, translation, tools, supplemental work and tax treatment. If a fee depends on sales or spend, request a worked example using your own data. Agree whether the base includes tax, shipping, refunds, coupons and platform-funded offers. A percentage of gross attributed sales can create different incentives from a percentage of settled sales or a fixed operating fee.

For example an illustrative quote at ¥220,000 monthly plus 7% of media costs ¥290,000 at ¥1 million of media. A ¥300,000 fixed quote looks dearer by ¥10,000. At ¥1.8 million of media, the variable quote becomes ¥346,000. The comparison still requires matching deliverables, authorship rights and effort. If the fixed quote excludes listing translations, fulfilment support or urgent promotion cover, include realistic add-ons for each bid. Use downside, baseline and expansion assumptions separately.

Score a work sample before committing a long retainer

Give both teams a small synthetic brief and the same anonymized ledger. Ask for a diagnosis of a discrepancy between attributed sales and cash receipts, a list of missing information and a 30-day plan. Score the reasoning, source checks, language quality, handover and ability to say when evidence is insufficient. Evaluate the people who will do the work, not only the pitch team. Pay for any substantial work sample and agree what you can retain.

Make essential risk controls pass or fail before applying a weighted score. A capable media team without a satisfactory approach to account ownership and access should not receive broad administrative permissions. Score answers about approvals, access, change logs and reimbursement separately from creative style. Ask the team to demonstrate how its monthly numbers trace to the source rather than displaying a reporting dashboard alone.

Keep account ownership under the business

A business-managed owner account with named agency roles makes changes and handover easier to manage. Document recovery emails, administrative rights, payment relationships and ownership of inventory data, ad accounts, source files, translations and creative licences. Avoid shared passwords where role-based access is supported. Agree which access is required while delivering the scope and how it will be changed on staff departures.

Put the exit drill in the proposal. Ask for an export and short handover guide after the first month, while communication is good. Validate that your own team can open the files, identify key changes and log in independently. If the agency holds media credits or long-lived data processing access, agree how it will transfer them or what cannot move.

Decide at day 60 based on evidence

During the first part of an engagement, require a baseline audit and a small documented improvement with clear ownership. In the second month review the quality of delivery, reporting, operating cadence and actual account access. Separate paid demand, changes to the offer and fulfilment. Ask whether weak results reflect failure of the test design or failure of the business hypothesis. Extend a pilot only to answer a remaining question with a clear spend limit.

Make the next contract match the work. A fixed diagnostic can be a good fit before recurring execution. Seasonal store operations may need surge capacity. Reporting problems may call for a data and finance specialist rather than a full agency. Expand access and scope as the partner proves ability to run the process. Keep a named internal owner even when execution is outsourced.

Decision exercises for your team

These scenarios are hypothetical. They do not describe actual clients, sellers or campaign results. Use the questions and sample inputs to review your own evidence, terms and operating limits.

Compare three proposals for a constrained pilot

Hypothetical scenario. A manufacturer receives one bid priced on media, one priced on net sales and one with a fixed operations fee.

Convert each quote into a low, base and high scenario using common assumptions and an explicit scope matrix. A low bid may depend on an unrealistically small workload or export retention. A variable fee may reward volume while refund and labour costs sit with the seller.

Prepare the review. To make this review possible, gather signed fee definition, caps and minimums, refund treatment, work hours, third-party expenses and stock obligations. Put the materials in the order the next reviewer will need them: the original evidence first, the interpretation second, and the decision third. Where two records disagree, keep both and identify who can supply the missing explanation.

For this case, shortlist against full cash costs and contracted outputs rather than quoted percentages. Identify the role that can stop the action and make sure the owner can see the evidence before the next commitment becomes irreversible.

Watch for this. Compare the same period, and separate once-only setup from recurring cost.

Write a brief that produces a meaningful work sample

Hypothetical scenario. A candidate claims it can fix marketplace performance but has not shown how it distinguishes discovery, listing, delivery and settlement issues.

Provide the same synthetic order and traffic data to each candidate and ask what it would investigate first. Strong candidates should state what cannot yet be inferred. A glossy strategy deck cannot substitute for a reproducible diagnosis of a concrete problem.

Challenge the conclusion. Look for anonymized synthetic dataset, evaluation criteria, time cap, example deliverable and rights arrangement. Distinguish facts the team can observe today from assumptions it will test in the next period. Ask a reviewer outside the original implementation to challenge the strongest explanation. If the team cannot reconstruct the sequence, record the issue as unresolved.

The proposed decision rule is to advance partners who can trace two outcomes and propose a bounded next test with a named owner. Record the earliest date the owner can make that decision and what activity should pause until the conditions are met.

Watch for this. Do not share customer personal information to make the exercise feel realistic.

Apportion rights to specific files

Hypothetical scenario. A vendor offers store photography, a layout tool and an editable catalogue under one price.

Ask who owns the originals, which items are licensed and what can be exported after termination. A brand might pay for the visible storefront while lacking usable editable assets or transferable fonts. Review rights individually instead of only asking who owns the website.

Keep the work reproducible. Use asset register, image licences, tool subscription conditions, account roles and export formats. Record which treatment applies, what changed during the observation and what part of the business was outside the scope. A dated snapshot lets another person compare the same conditions after the process or platform changes.

At the review, contract for the assets that must remain usable after handover. Give the next owner a brief note on the decision, the facts that support it and one finding that would warrant reopening it.

Watch for this. For assets that cannot transfer, record the practical replacement cost before signing.

Escalate an operations failure while protecting customer information

Hypothetical scenario. An incorrect inventory file causes orders the warehouse cannot fulfil.

Assign one approvals owner, stop continuing exposure and send the support team only the information it needs. Contract labels rarely explain who acts during a service failure. A rehearsal should cover correction, consumer communication and settlement.

Open the review with evidence. Ask the responsible team to bring change log, stock feed history, permission list, fulfilment capacity and affected order references. Compare the record with what the buyer or operator actually experienced. Keep the original terms alongside any model so a new reviewer can distinguish differences in scope from errors in arithmetic or reporting.

For this case, cap access and clarify authority for stock and promotion changes before the next campaign. Identify the role that can stop the action and make sure the owner can see the evidence before the next commitment becomes irreversible.

Watch for this. Use order reference identifiers sparingly and avoid sharing unnecessary data across vendors.

Run a clean end-of-contract handover

Hypothetical scenario. The current agency retained most knowledge about edited listings and promotional rules.

Conduct a limited handover while the contract is still active and validate the files in a buyer-controlled account. The replacement team may otherwise repeat costly work or accidentally alter historical reporting. Make the transition reproducible.

Prepare the review. To make this review possible, gather latest editable assets, ad history, product exports, owner and billing details and a list of known gaps. Put the materials in the order the next reviewer will need them: the original evidence first, the interpretation second, and the decision third. Where two records disagree, keep both and identify who can supply the missing explanation.

The proposed decision rule is to collect exports and written operating procedures before administrative access is disabled. Record the earliest date the owner can make that decision and what activity should pause until the conditions are met.

Watch for this. Remove seller credentials or tokens from handover documents.

Check reporting for causal overreach

Hypothetical scenario. A proposal promises a proportion of revenue uplift after changing creative, price and advertising at the same time.

Ask for an experiment design, a plausible comparison and a finance definition of contribution. Simultaneous changes can be commercially justified, yet the combined effect cannot be assigned precisely to one action.

Challenge the conclusion. Look for scope timeline, historical baseline, order cohorts, stock dates, prices and platform attribution settings. Distinguish facts the team can observe today from assumptions it will test in the next period. Ask a reviewer outside the original implementation to challenge the strongest explanation. If the team cannot reconstruct the sequence, record the issue as unresolved.

At the review, score the partner on how it describes uncertainty and tracks operational outcomes. Give the next owner a brief note on the decision, the facts that support it and one finding that would warrant reopening it.

Watch for this. A guarantee based on insufficient evidence is not a useful proxy for ability.

Protect continuity through a staff change

Hypothetical scenario. The lead operator named in the pitch leaves before a peak campaign.

Compare the proposal's staff allocation and training promise with who actually holds access and owns each deliverable. One person holding all knowledge creates a continuity problem. Assign ownership to roles, with named people and measurable evidence that a successor can deliver.

Keep the work reproducible. Use delivery roster, backup owners, training guides, review calendar and escalation route. Record which treatment applies, what changed during the observation and what part of the business was outside the scope. A dated snapshot lets another person compare the same conditions after the process or platform changes.

For this case, request a rehearsed handoff before assigning the new team high-risk changes. Identify the role that can stop the action and make sure the owner can see the evidence before the next commitment becomes irreversible.

Watch for this. Role changes must trigger an access review in addition to a project staffing change.

Create a proposal comparison the finance team can use

Define must-pass items first: legal seller access remains usable by the business, agency roles are named, records can be exported, and critical changes require an approval path. Then compare capability. An illustrative scoring matrix can assign 25 points to marketplace operations, 20 to quality of the submitted diagnosis, 20 to measurement and reconciliation, 15 to Japanese content and support, 10 to capacity and continuity, and 10 to the expected economics. This is a decision aid. A score cannot compensate for failure of a required control.

Ask each candidate to supply one complete proposed deliverable. For a reporting service, request an order reconciliation and assumptions. For catalog work, request editable Japanese copy, scope of product information and an approval workflow. For full store operations, ask how the team would handle an outage when a campaign is active. A proposal that gives a different answer to each role has useful depth. A generic sheet of platform logos tells little about how work is carried out.

Negotiate the ambiguous cases that create unexpected fees

Specify whether monthly charges include a given number of SKUs and changes, revisions, language review, holiday service and data exports. Define urgent issues in business terms and specify what happens if a platform claim is rejected. Agree how a media minimum behaves in a month with unexpectedly low stock. Make sure supplier, shipping and account access delays can be distinguished from agency delays. Ask the vendor to show where it will ask for a new approval rather than bill automatically.

A bank reconciliation can make an apparently minor fee ambiguity material. If € or US dollar costs are converted to yen, record the settlement rate or agreed conversion approach. If tax and pass-through media are included, show them separately. A comparison that misses the difference between seller revenue and attributed gross sales will become hard to audit after returns begin. Attach a real or illustrative calculation to the statement of work.

Answer questions before selecting an agency

Can the agency manage the shop in its own account? Have the legal account owner and responsible operator review the implications. Ask which roles and recovery channels belong to your business and which are legitimately held by a partner. Require a usable exit plan before granting administrative access.

How do I compare an agency that offers consulting with one that offers execution? Separate the desired outputs. An advisory brief can be excellent for diagnosis and ownership. A store operating scope must cover routine changes, customer exceptions and qualified reviewers. Compare evidence for the work you actually need.

What should be in the first review? Bring the original hypothesis, operational changes since launch, cash spent, completed deliverables, known data gaps and a decision to continue, change scope or stop. Give both teams a chance to correct factual errors before scoring outcomes.

Make the transition between insight and execution explicit

A diagnosis can be right and still fail if it hands recommendations to a team without the necessary access, language support or operating capacity. Ask who will turn the recommended change into a ticket, who approves it, and how long support and finance have to prepare. For example, changing fulfillment claims on a product page may require a carrier confirmation and an updated service response. Record ticket ownership before agreeing the implementation date.

Give the next reviewer a change log that lists what is live, planned and paused. A replacement agency should be able to reconcile current scope and permissions without being given unlimited access. Keep tasks with regulatory or customer-data consequences subject to the business's own approval process.

Review the contract against three operating incidents

Ask each candidate to explain the same three incidents before comparing contract language. First, an inventory feed lists a product as available after the last saleable unit is reserved. Second, an order uses a coupon whose funding does not match the seller’s forecast. Third, a product claim published on a marketplace requires correction after new evidence arrives. In each incident ask who discovers the issue, who has the authority to limit further exposure, who records what happened, how the customer is told, and which costs and records the business can see. The contract should establish a usable escalation path alongside the written allocation of work.

Ask qualified advisers to review liability, confidentiality, data handling, intellectual property and termination using the actual agreement and roles. The operating team can do useful work before that review by describing the consequences of each choice. For example, a provision giving a seller the right to export account reports is more useful when the seller can identify which files it needs, on what schedule and in which format. If an agency administers a third-party tool, demonstrate how access changes when a user leaves or a contract ends. Rights to records, rights to edit creative, and access to the marketplace account deserve distinct answers.

Evaluate references without implying an endorsement

Ask a candidate to offer relevant examples it can share with permission, including the specific work performed and its dates. A storefront visible in public can show how a product range is presented, but it does not confirm which vendor produced it or what that vendor achieved. An agency may have improved account operations while another team controlled pricing, stock or advertising. An anonymized example is often sufficient for a work sample if the candidate explains its role, the initial problem and what evidence supports the result. Avoid requiring unredacted customer data, contracts or proprietary reports.

When reviewing a reference conversation, allow the reference to describe what the engagement can fairly demonstrate. Ask how ownership, day-to-day quality and reporting changed, what happened under pressure and which tasks required substantial work from the business. Avoid turning a positive reference into a general guarantee. For a direct comparison, record the questions you asked and the limits of each example. A store launch and a mature optimization contract answer different questions even if both relate to the same marketplace.

Set a time and criterion for taking control back

Agree early what happens when the scope needs to change. A business may want to bring creative in-house, add a second agency for campaigns, or switch operators after a quiet period. Write down how account roles, unit economics models and approval records will be handed over under each scenario. Make the transition depend on an approved record of critical issues and accessible assets. Assign backup individuals and specify when to transfer responsibilities for customer enquiries, ongoing reports and already approved offers. A handover that is easy to rehearse early will be easier to execute during an urgent change.
