Pricing & Commercial Strategy

Pricing is a strategic marketing decision that determines how value is captured and how the brand is positioned in the market.

Pricing approaches

  • Cost-based — price derived from cost plus target margin. Simple but ignores customer value and competitive context.
  • Competitor-based — price set relative to rivals. Useful for parity categories; dangerous when the brand has genuine differentiation.
  • Value-based — price reflects the value the customer receives. Requires deep understanding of customer economics and willingness to pay.
  • Premium — deliberately high price that signals quality, scarcity or status and supports brand positioning.
  • Penetration — low initial price to gain volume and share, with later increases or monetisation through other means.
  • Psychological — use of charm pricing, anchoring, bundling and framing to influence perceived value.
  • Dynamic — price adjusts with demand, inventory or customer segment (common in travel and digital).
  • Subscription and bundling — recurring revenue and package structures that change the customer’s value calculation.

Architecture and economics

Good-better-best tiering, product-line pricing and clear role for each price point reduce random discounting and help customers self-select. Gross margin, contribution margin, customer acquisition cost (CAC) and customer lifetime value (LTV) must be examined together. Revenue growth that is bought with heavy discounting or high CAC can destroy value even while top-line figures look healthy.

Hypothetical illustration (clearly labelled)

Suppose a product costs £40 to produce and distribute, sells at £100, and acquires a customer for £60 in marketing. First-order contribution after acquisition is £0. Only repeat purchases or lower future CAC make the relationship profitable. This is a simplified numerical example for illustration; real businesses must use their own cost and behavioural data.

Key research questions

  • What is the current price architecture and how does it map to customer segments?
  • How dependent is volume on promotional pricing?
  • What is the relationship between CAC and LTV for major segments?
  • Where would a 5–10% price increase or decrease most affect volume and margin?
