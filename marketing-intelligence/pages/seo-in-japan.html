Purpose: A practical reference for international businesses that want to become discoverable when Japanese customers research products, services and solutions. It is educational, not a service offer.

A practical guide to Japanese search behaviour, keyword research, website localization, technical SEO, content strategy and sustainable organic growth.

Entering a new country is more than translating a website, opening a local office or advertising a product to a new audience. Businesses entering Japan need to understand how potential customers discover information, evaluate suppliers, compare products and make purchasing decisions.

Search engine optimization (SEO) can help international businesses become discoverable when Japanese customers are researching products, services and solutions. It is not simply a process of translating existing English articles into Japanese and waiting for Google to deliver customers.

If traffic rises while qualification falls, inspect query intent and the offer before funding more content. See Google’s SEO Starter Guide and its documentation for localized pages.

1. Understanding the Japanese search environment

Before investing in SEO, businesses should understand the search environment in which they intend to compete. Japan has a mature digital economy, established search habits and a large population of internet users. However, a business should not assume that the search behaviour of Japanese customers is identical to that of customers in its existing markets.

1.1 Google and the importance of Google Search

Google Search should be a central consideration when developing an SEO strategy for Japan. Businesses should begin with Google’s official Search Essentials and SEO Starter Guide rather than relying on outdated SEO techniques or unsupported ranking promises.

Google’s guidance covers three important areas:

  • Technical requirements that allow Google to access and understand a website.
  • Policies describing practices that can lead to content being excluded or negatively affected.
  • Recommended practices for creating useful, discoverable web content.

Practical recommendation: Set up Google Search Console before launching a major Japanese SEO campaign. It helps website owners monitor search performance, identify indexing issues and understand the queries through which their pages appear in Google Search.

1.2 Do not assume that English search behaviour translates into Japanese

One of the most important differences international companies need to consider is language. A company might use the phrase “project management software” in its English marketing materials. A Japanese customer could search using several different expressions depending on the product, business situation and problem being solved.

Potential Japanese expressions include:

  • プロジェクト管理ツール
  • プロジェクト管理システム
  • タスク管理ツール
  • 業務管理システム

These phrases are related, but they are not necessarily interchangeable. If an international company translates only its primary English keyword, it could miss important search opportunities. The better approach is to understand the language used by Japanese customers and organize keywords according to their actual needs.

1.3 Understand the role of Japanese writing systems

Japanese uses three principal writing systems:

  • Hiragana (ひらがな) — used for native Japanese words, grammatical elements and other parts of written Japanese.
  • Katakana (カタカナ) — commonly used for foreign-origin words, loanwords, product terminology and some technical expressions.
  • Kanji (漢字) — characters used in Japanese vocabulary that often carry important meaning within words and phrases.

A single product or service may be described using different combinations of Japanese characters, katakana, English abbreviations and industry terminology. For example, a business researching search engine optimization might encounter: SEO, 検索エンジン最適化, SEO対策, SEOコンサルティング. These expressions may attract different audiences and indicate different levels of knowledge or commercial interest.

Search intent describes what a person hopes to accomplish when entering a query. Four useful categories are:

These categories are useful for organizing content, but actual search intent must be investigated for each keyword. A business should not assume that every query containing the word SEO is a request to hire an SEO agency.

2. Why international businesses need Japanese keyword research

Keyword research is the process of identifying and evaluating the search expressions that potential customers use. It helps businesses understand the questions people ask, the terminology they recognize and the information they expect to find. For companies entering Japan, keyword research should be treated as market research as well as an SEO activity.

2.1 Start with the customer’s problem, not the translated keyword

Consider an international accounting software company planning to sell its product in Japan. Its English marketing team might begin with keywords such as “accounting software”, “online accounting” or “financial management software”. A direct translation would be a useful starting point, but it would not be sufficient.

Potential Japanese expressions include:

  • 会計ソフト
  • クラウド会計ソフト
  • 会計システム
  • 経理業務 効率化

The objective is not to collect the largest possible keyword list. It is to identify the language that connects the product to a genuine customer problem.

2.2 Build a Japanese keyword research process

A practical keyword research project can follow these steps:

Step 1: Define the target customer

Identify who the business wants to reach — for example Japanese SMEs, enterprise procurement teams, software developers, ecommerce operators, or international companies already operating in Japan. Each audience can use different terminology.

Step 2: List the problems your product solves

Do not start with your product catalogue alone. Consider the situations that lead someone to look for a solution (missed deadlines, unclear responsibilities, poor visibility into progress, etc.).

Step 3: Research Japanese expressions

Use several sources: Google Keyword Planner, Google Search autocomplete, related searches, Google Trends, competitor websites, Japanese industry publications, customer interviews, and questions received by Japanese sales representatives.

Step 4: Group related keywords

Keyword group Example expressions Content opportunity
General SEO SEO · 日本 SEO Educational guide
SEO services SEO対策 · SEOコンサルティング Service information
Technical SEO テクニカルSEO · サイト速度 Technical guide
Local SEO ローカルSEO · 店舗 SEO Local search guide
SEO costs SEO 料金 · SEO 費用 Cost explanation

Step 5: Examine the actual search results

Search each important expression in Google and review the pages that appear. Ask whether the results are mainly educational articles, agency service pages, product comparison sites, or official resources — and what information appears to be missing.

Step 6: Prioritize keywords

Evaluate each keyword according to relevance to the product and target customer, search intent, commercial importance, ability to create genuinely useful content, and competition. A keyword with fewer searches may be more commercially valuable than a broad keyword with a large audience.

2.3 Use Google Search Console to improve keyword decisions

After publishing Japanese content, Google Search Console becomes an important source of first-party search performance information. Businesses can examine queries generating impressions, clicks, average position, pages receiving visibility, and technical indexing issues. This is a continuous research process, not a one-time keyword exercise.

3. Website localization: More than translating English into Japanese

Website localization is one of the most important considerations for international businesses entering Japan. Translation changes the language of the content. Localization adapts the experience to the audience. A technically accurate translation can still feel unfamiliar to Japanese visitors if the terminology, examples, product explanations or communication style are not appropriate.

3.1 Use professional Japanese language review

Machine translation and AI-assisted translation can help create an initial draft, but they should not replace proper language review. A Japanese-language reviewer should check grammar, natural vocabulary, industry-specific terminology, product descriptions, tone and formality, calls to action, and cultural and contextual accuracy.

3.2 Adapt the examples to Japanese customers

An American company publishing an article about employee management software might use examples based entirely on US employment practices. A Japanese reader may find the explanation less relevant if it does not address the reader’s local business environment. The information should be understandable and relevant to the people who are expected to use it.

3.3 Create separate English and Japanese pages

If a business serves international and Japanese audiences, it should provide clear language-specific versions of its website, for example example.com/en/ and example.com/ja/. Each language version should have its own appropriate page title, a natural language-specific URL, correct language declarations, relevant localized headings, language-appropriate internal links, and a clear language-selection option.

3.4 Implement hreflang correctly

The hreflang attribute helps search engines understand the intended language and regional audience of alternate pages. A simplified implementation might look like this: 

<link rel="alternate" hreflang="en"
      href="https://example.com/en/seo-in-japan/" />
<link rel="alternate" hreflang="ja"
      href="https://example.com/ja/japan-seo/" />
<link rel="alternate" hreflang="x-default"
      href="https://example.com/seo-in-japan/" />

Each language version should reference itself. Alternate language pages should reference one another. Language codes should follow accepted standards. Do not use automatic IP-based redirects as the only way to expose language alternatives.

3.5 Design a useful language-selection experience

A language switcher should be easy to find (for example English | 日本語). When a visitor selects Japanese on an article page, the website should take them to the corresponding Japanese article where one exists — not automatically to the Japanese homepage.

4. Creating Japanese content that earns trust

A business entering Japan should not publish content simply because a keyword appears to have search potential. The content should help readers understand something important, solve a problem or make a better decision. This is especially important for international companies that have not yet established a strong reputation in Japan.

4.1 Understand what a useful article should accomplish

A weak cybersecurity article might simply describe why cybersecurity is important and repeatedly mention the company’s product. A stronger article could explain common risks, how organizations can assess their existing practices, what questions to ask before selecting a provider, how to evaluate solutions, and where to find authoritative technical guidance — while still explaining where the product fits.

4.2 Demonstrate experience and expertise

Readers should be able to understand:

  • Who wrote the article and what relevant experience the author has
  • Who reviewed technical information
  • Which sources support important claims
  • When the content was last substantively reviewed
  • How readers can contact the organization

Never invent credentials, customer projects, company histories or case studies. If an article is written with assistance from AI, it should still receive meaningful human research, fact-checking, editorial review and appropriate expert verification.

4.4 Avoid unsupported claims

Statements such as “every Japanese customer prefers Japanese-language websites”, “a particular keyword guarantees new customers”, or “a certain number of backlinks guarantees first-page rankings” should not appear without reliable evidence. Good educational content acknowledges differences between industries, customers and business situations.

5. Technical SEO for Japanese websites

Even excellent content can struggle to appear in search results if search engines cannot access or understand the website. Technical SEO focuses on the structure and functionality that allow search engines and visitors to use a website effectively.

5.1 Website structure and URL planning

A simple structure might be:

example.com/
├── en/
│   ├── seo-in-japan/
│   ├── japan-market-entry/
│   └── digital-marketing-japan/
└── ja/
    ├── japan-seo/
    ├── japan-market-entry/
    └── digital-marketing/

5.2 Choose the right domain structure

Option Example Notes
Subdirectories example.com/en/ · example.com/ja/ One primary domain, shared management, clear language separation. Often a practical starting point.
Subdomains en.example.com · ja.example.com Separate technical configurations and independent hosting options possible.
Country-specific domain example.jp Strong geographical signal, but check eligibility, registration requirements and long-term management.

5.3 Make important content accessible to search engines

  • Public pages return appropriate HTTP responses
  • Important content is not blocked by robots.txt or unintended noindex directives
  • Internal links use crawlable HTML links
  • Canonical URLs and XML sitemaps are configured correctly
  • JavaScript does not prevent important content from being discovered

5.4 Optimize mobile usability & page performance

Japanese customers may discover and access websites through smartphones, computers and tablets. Check text readability, navigation, form usability, image sizing, page loading performance, button spacing and language-switching functionality. Use PageSpeed Insights and Google Search Console to investigate Core Web Vitals rather than relying only on subjective impressions.

5.5 Implement structured data where appropriate

Relevant schema types may include Organization, Article, BreadcrumbList, Product and LocalBusiness (where applicable). Structured data must accurately represent visible page content and should not be used to create misleading claims.

6. Developing a Japanese content strategy

A successful content strategy should connect informational articles, product explanations and useful service pages. International businesses should organize content around the questions their potential customers ask at different stages of their research.

6.1 Build topic clusters

A topic cluster consists of a central guide supported by related articles. For example, a company offering SEO services could build a pillar page “SEO in Japan” supported by articles on Japanese keyword research, natural content writing, technical SEO checklists, local SEO, localization considerations, measurement, common mistakes, and how to evaluate an agency.

6.2 Create content for different customer stages

Customer stage Example question Recommended content
Awareness How does SEO work in Japan? Educational guide
Research How do I research Japanese keywords? Practical tutorial
Evaluation What should I look for in an SEO provider? Evaluation checklist
Planning How much work does Japanese SEO require? Planning guide
Decision How can I discuss my website’s needs? Contact information

6.3 Publish original examples & build a resource library

Generic explanations are available across thousands of websites. Original examples help readers understand how information applies to real situations. Over time, the website could offer genuinely useful resources such as a Japanese SEO audit checklist, localization checklist, keyword research worksheet, or performance reporting template — without requiring unnecessary personal information.

7. Local SEO in Japan

Local SEO is particularly important for companies serving customers in specific Japanese cities or regions. A business should only claim locations where it has a genuine business presence and can meet the relevant eligibility requirements.

7.1 Google Business Profile

Google Business Profile allows eligible businesses to manage their business information across Google Search and Maps. Provide accurate information and follow Google’s eligibility guidelines. Do not create multiple profiles for imaginary offices or use misleading addresses to target additional cities.

7.2 Consistent business information & genuinely local content

A company’s identity should be consistent across its website and legitimate business directories (name, address, telephone, website, category). Local content should answer real questions about the area served rather than simply replacing the name of one city with another across dozens of identical pages.

8. Building authority and earning relevant links

Backlinks can help search engines discover pages and understand relationships between websites. Businesses should focus on earning relevant references rather than purchasing large quantities of artificial links.

Create resources worth referencing (researched guides, practical glossaries, original industry surveys, localization checklists) and develop genuine industry relationships with trade organizations, professional associations, industry publications and relevant communities. Avoid manipulative link-building practices that violate Google’s spam policies.

9. Measuring Japanese SEO performance

SEO should be evaluated using meaningful business and website performance measurements. A company should not assume that a higher ranking automatically means greater commercial success.

Measurement Why it matters

Relevant landing pages Content performance
Engaged sessions Visitor engagement
Contact enquiries / qualified leads Business relevance
Conversions Commercial outcomes

Use Google Search Console for search performance and Google Analytics 4 for visitor behaviour and configured conversions. Establish a realistic reporting schedule (monthly technical and performance reviews, quarterly content assessments). Do not promise first-page rankings within a fixed number of days.

10. Common Japanese SEO mistakes made by international companies

  1. Translating everything literally — Research terminology with Japanese-language sources and native-level reviewers instead.
  2. Targeting only English keywords — Develop a separate Japanese keyword research process.
  3. Publishing machine-translated articles without review — Use professional Japanese editing and subject-matter review.
  4. Ignoring Japanese customer needs — Adapt examples, terminology and explanations to the intended audience.
  5. Treating SEO as a one-time activity — Establish a regular editorial and technical review process.
  6. Focusing entirely on rankings — Connect SEO measurements with relevant enquiries and business outcomes.
  7. Creating unnecessary pages — Prioritize useful, distinct content that addresses real search intent.
  8. Ignoring website credibility — Publish accurate company information, author details, contact information and transparent policies.

11. A practical 90-day Japanese SEO preparation plan

The following is an illustrative plan for an international company starting its Japanese SEO work. It is not a guarantee of ranking or traffic growth.

Days 1–30: Research and preparation

  • Week 1: Define the intended Japanese customer, relevant products/services, problems solved, competitors and industry terminology.
  • Week 2: Collect Japanese keyword variations, review search intent and current results, group and prioritize.
  • Week 3: Decide English/Japanese URL structure, navigation, language switcher, canonical and hreflang requirements; create initial sitemap.
  • Week 4: Write the main Japanese SEO strategy page, identify qualified reviewers, prepare author profiles and editorial guidelines.

Days 31–60: Build and publish

  • Week 5: Configure Google Search Console, check indexing accessibility, mobile usability and page performance.
  • Week 6: Publish the main Japanese-language landing page and first educational guide (plus corresponding English version).
  • Week 7: Develop supporting resources (keyword research guide, localization checklist, problem-focused article).
  • Week 8: Review language quality, mobile presentation, contact forms, links, references and indexing status.

Days 61–90: Improve and learn

  • Week 9: Review Search Console impressions, new queries, landing-page performance and indexing issues.
  • Week 10: Improve content — add missing explanations, improve headings, add relevant examples, correct inaccuracies.
  • Week 11: Share educational resources with relevant business communities and seek feedback.
  • Week 12: Identify the most useful content, review engagement and enquiries, prioritize the next group of articles and set the next 90-day plan.

13. Frequently asked questions about SEO in Japan

1. Is SEO different in Japan compared with other countries?

The fundamental principles remain similar: websites need to be accessible, relevant, useful and understandable. However, language, search terminology, customer expectations, competition and localization requirements can differ significantly. Research the Japanese audience rather than assuming strategies from another market will transfer unchanged.

2. Is Google the only search engine businesses should consider in Japan?

Google should be a major priority for most international businesses. Businesses should also understand the wider digital discovery environment of their target customers, including platforms, marketplaces and industry-specific resources.

3. Should I create a Japanese version of my English website?

If Japanese customers are an important target audience, a professionally localized Japanese website is generally worth considering. The decision should also account for the company’s ability to maintain accurate Japanese content and provide appropriate customer support.

4. Can I rank an English website in Google Japan?

Yes. English-language pages can appear in Japanese search results when relevant. However, an English page may not satisfy someone who wants Japanese-language information. Creating useful Japanese content is usually more appropriate when the intended audience is primarily Japanese-speaking.

5. Do I need a .jp domain for Japanese SEO?

No. A .jp domain is not an absolute requirement. A business can organize Japanese-language content on a generic domain using a dedicated directory such as /ja/. The choice should reflect branding, technical requirements, audience and long-term plans.

6. How much does Japanese SEO cost?

There is no universal price. Cost depends on keyword research, website localization, technical SEO, content production, professional translation and editing, link earning, reporting and ongoing optimization. Request a clearly defined scope of work before comparing service prices.

7. How long does Japanese SEO take?

There is no guaranteed timeline. Results depend on the website’s existing condition, competition, content quality, technical implementation and resources. Establish realistic milestones and evaluate progress regularly.

8. Can I use AI to translate my website into Japanese?

AI can assist with translation and drafting, but the final content should be reviewed for accuracy, natural language and industry terminology. A Japanese reader should not have to struggle through awkward translations or incorrect technical explanations.

9. What is hreflang, and why does it matter?

hreflang is an HTML or sitemap signal that helps search engines understand alternative language or regional versions of a page. It is particularly useful when a company publishes English and Japanese versions of equivalent content. Incorrect implementation can create confusion.

10. How can I measure the success of Japanese SEO?

Use a combination of search visibility, relevant website traffic and business outcomes. Google Search Console provides search performance information; Google Analytics 4 can help measure visitor activity and configured conversions. For a B2B company, qualified enquiries may be more important than total website visits.

11. Should I hire a Japanese SEO agency?

It depends on internal capabilities. A business may benefit from a specialist when it lacks local language expertise, technical SEO experience or knowledge of the Japanese search environment. Evaluate experience, methods, communication, reporting and approach to content quality. Avoid providers promising guaranteed rankings.

12. Is local SEO important for foreign companies in Japan?

It can be important for businesses serving customers in particular locations. Local SEO should reflect a genuine business presence or eligible service-area operation. A foreign company serving customers exclusively online may need a different approach from a company with a physical office in Japan.

Conclusion: Build your Japanese search presence on understanding and trust

Entering Japan is a significant business decision. International companies need more than a translated website to establish a meaningful online presence. They need to understand the people they want to reach, the language those people use, the problems they are trying to solve and the information they need before choosing a supplier.

Japanese SEO should therefore begin with research and understanding. Start by defining your target audience. Investigate Japanese search terminology. Review the existing search results. Create useful localized content. Make sure the website is technically accessible. Provide clear information about the business and its expertise. Measure performance and improve the website as you learn more about your audience.

Most importantly, avoid treating SEO as a shortcut to instant rankings. A sustainable search presence develops through consistent work, useful information, technical reliability and a genuine commitment to helping customers.

Useful information should come before a sales conversation. That is the foundation of a strong long-term presence in Japan.
